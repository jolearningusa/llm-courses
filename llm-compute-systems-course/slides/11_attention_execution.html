5 / 17

Online state

m = row maximum; l = Σ exp(score − m)
u = Σ exp(score − m) × value

The normalized result is u/l. The weighted sum must be rescaled when the running maximum changes.

6 / 17

Merge two score tiles

m′ = max(m, tile_max)
l′ = exp(m−m′)l + Σexp(tile_score−m′)

Apply the same old-state scale to u, then add the tile’s weighted values.

7 / 17

Worked merge

Scores [0, ln 2], scalar values [2, 5]. After the first tile: m=0, l=1, u=2.

After the second: m=ln 2, old scale=1/2, l=1.5, u=6.

output = 6 / 1.5 = 4

Same result as weights [1/3, 2/3] over the two values.

10 / 17

What becomes smaller

Avoiding a full materialized score/probability matrix reduces intermediate storage and traffic. Dense attention still performs work over the permitted query/key pairs; tiling does not remove its quadratic prefill arithmetic.

Backward and recomputation

Backward needs information derived from forward. Storing less can require recomputation; track work and saved-state lifetime together. A forward-only comparison is insufficient for training.

Decode is a different regime

A new query may attend to many retained keys. KV layout, available parallelism and reduction overhead can matter more than a large-prefill attention strategy.

Ragged requests

Equal total tokens can still produce uneven tile work when lengths differ. Planning and scheduling can affect the critical path even with a fast microkernel.
