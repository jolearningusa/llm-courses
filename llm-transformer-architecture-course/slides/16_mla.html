MLA: multi-head latent attention

DeepSeek-AI, 2024 (DeepSeek-V2). Cache one small latent vector per token instead of per-head keys and values, and rebuild the heads from it. A smaller cache than GQA, with quality the paper reports as better than multi-head attention.

Problem: sharing heads trades away quality

  • MQA and GQA (decks 12–13) shrink the cache by making heads share keys and values. Fewer distinct KV heads, some quality lost.
  • DeepSeek-V2 targets 128K context and cheap serving of a 236B-parameter MoE model (21B active per token). Its predecessor, DeepSeek 67B, used a much larger cache.
  • Question: can the cache be compressed instead of shared, so every head keeps its own keys and values?

Change: low-rank joint compression of K and V

  • Only the latent ctKV is cached (plus a small position key, slide 5). Per-head keys and values are up-projected from it.
  • Queries are compressed too (to d′c = 1,536). That saves activation memory in training, not cache.

Absorb the up-projections at inference

qᵀk = (WQh)ᵀ(WUKc) = ((WUK)ᵀWQh)ᵀ c

  • Matrix products associate: WUK can be folded into the query projection, and WUV into the output projection.
  • At decoding time the model scores queries directly against the cached latents and never materializes per-head keys or values.
  • Lab L5 checks that the absorbed and the explicit computation give the same scores.

Change: decoupled RoPE

  • RoPE (deck 08) multiplies keys by a position-dependent rotation. Placed between WQ and WUK, that rotation blocks the absorption above, and keys for all past tokens would have to be recomputed at every step.
  • Fix: carry position in a separate, small part. Each head gets an extra query part qR of width dhR = 64, and all heads share one rotated key kR of width 64.
  • Score = content part (through the latent) + position part (through RoPE). The cache holds cKV and kR: 512 + 64 = 576 numbers per token per layer.

Evidence

  • Against DeepSeek 67B: KV cache reduced by 93.3%, maximum generation throughput 5.76×, and training cost 42.5% lower (172.8K against 300.6K GPU hours per trillion tokens).
  • The paper states MLA "achieves better performance than MHA" while caching far less, with the ablations in its appendix.
  • The 93.3% and 5.76× compare whole models (MLA, the MoE layers and, in deployment, KV-cache quantization), not attention alone. Read them as a system result.

Modeling impact

  • Compress, do not share. The cache becomes a learned bottleneck; every head still has distinct keys and values.
  • Arithmetic moves to decoding. Scoring against a 576-wide latent with absorbed matrices is more compute per cached byte than GQA, which suits memory-bound decoding.
  • Positions need their own channel. Any scheme that folds matrices across the cache must keep position-dependent parts separate.
  • Harder to implement. Kernels and serving stacks needed MLA-specific paths, which the compute course covers.

Afterlife

  • DeepSeek-V3 and R1 keep MLA with the same latent sizes (deck 31).
  • Kimi K2 adopts the DeepSeek-V3 architecture, MLA included, with fewer attention heads (deck 33).
  • DeepSeek-V3.2 adds a sparse attention selector on top of MLA (deck 26).
  • Most other families stayed with GQA, which needs no special kernels.
