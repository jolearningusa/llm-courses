Chameleon: early fusion

Chameleon Team (Meta), 2024. Turn images into discrete tokens, put them in the same vocabulary as text, and train one Transformer from scratch on interleaved sequences. It can generate images and text in any order.

Problem: late fusion reads images but cannot write them

  • Flamingo and LLaVA (deck 36) attach a separate vision encoder to a text LM. The LM sees continuous image features but outputs only text.
  • Generating images then needs a separate model, and documents with images and text interleaved are hard to produce in one pass.
  • Goal: one model, one token stream, both directions, trained from the start on mixed data.

Change: images as tokens

  • The image tokenizer (after Gafni et al. 2022) is a vector-quantized autoencoder: each 512 × 512 image becomes 1,024 codes from a codebook of 8,192.
  • A new BPE tokenizer has 65,536 entries, including the 8,192 image codes.
  • No separate encoders or decoders in the Transformer: the same next-token loss trains text and image tokens.

Change: keeping mixed-modal training stable

  • Mixed-modal training diverged late in training. The cause: output norms grew and logits drifted, because the softmax is invariant to adding a constant and modalities "compete" through norm growth.
  • Watching the norm of the output predicted a divergence before the loss showed it.
  • Fixes: QK-Norm in both models (deck 30); dropout and z-loss for 7B; for 34B, a Swin-style norm reordering (normalize the sub-layer output before adding it to the residual) and z-loss without dropout.

Evidence

  • Mixed-modal generation (human evaluation): Chameleon-34B's responses were preferred over Gemini Pro's 69.1% of the time and over GPT-4V's 61.7% of the time; against those models augmented with generated images, 60.4% and 51.6%.
  • Image captioning: 140.8 CIDEr on COCO and 82.3 on Flickr30k (SFT model), the best among the models compared.
  • Text only: beats Llama 2 70B on 5 of 8 commonsense tasks, on par with Mixtral 8x7B; MMLU 65.8 against 70.6 for Mixtral and 71.8 for Gemini Pro.

Modeling impact

  • Fusion is a design axis. Late fusion (adapters, projections) is cheap and reuses LMs; early fusion trains one model on all modalities from the start and can generate them all.
  • Discrete tokenizers decide quality. A VQ tokenizer sets the ceiling for image reconstruction, as audio codecs do for speech (decks 41–42).
  • Stability tricks spread. QK-Norm and output-norm monitoring from mixed-modal training became standard in text models (Qwen3, Gemma 3, OLMo 2).
  • Industry follow-up. Llama 4 describes itself as natively multimodal with early fusion (deck 29), though it uses a vision encoder rather than VQ tokens for input.
