Problem: Post-LN needs a learning-rate warm-up

  • The 2017 Transformer normalizes after each residual addition (Post-LN). Training it needs a warm-up: a learning rate that starts near zero and grows over thousands of steps.
  • Without warm-up, Adam on IWSLT14 De-En reached about 8.45 BLEU against about 34 with warm-up.
  • Warm-up slows training and adds hyperparameters, and nobody had a clear account of why it was needed.

Why it works: gradients at initialization

  • Post-LN: the expected gradient of the last FFN layer's weights is O(d √(ln d)), independent of depth L. Large gradients near the output make a large learning rate unstable, so warm-up is needed.
  • Pre-LN: the same gradient is O(d √(ln d / L)); it shrinks with depth, and gradients are well behaved across layers.
  • Side effect: in Pre-LN the hidden-state norm grows linearly with depth, because nothing normalizes the residual stream. Hence the final LayerNorm before the output.
Xiong et al. 2020, arXiv:2002.04745 §3.3, Lemma 2, Theorem 1 · bibliography
4 / 10

Evidence: Pre-LN without warm-up

  • Translation: Pre-LN without warm-up matches Post-LN with warm-up in BLEU on IWSLT14 De-En and WMT14 En-De, and converges faster; on IWSLT its 9th checkpoint matches Post-LN's 15th.
  • BERT pre-training: the authors report a 40% speed-up to the same validation loss, with no warm-up and a larger learning rate (3e-4 against 1e-4).
  • Fine-tuned on MRPC and RTE, the Pre-LN model also converged faster.

RMSNorm: drop the mean

āi = ai / RMS(a) · gi , RMS(a) = √( (1/n) Σ ai² )

  • LayerNorm re-centers (subtracts the mean) and re-scales (divides by the standard deviation). RMSNorm keeps only re-scaling, and has no bias.
  • Hypothesis: re-scaling invariance is what makes LayerNorm work; re-centering is not needed.
  • When the mean of the inputs is zero, RMSNorm and LayerNorm are the same.

Modeling impact

  • Depth and learning rate. Pre-LN lets deep stacks train with large learning rates; warm-up remains common in practice, but it is no longer the thing holding training together.
  • The residual stream is a highway. With Pre-LN each layer adds to an un-normalized sum; the final norm is required, and later layers write into an ever-larger stream.
  • Cheaper and simpler. RMSNorm removes a reduction and a bias per normalization; a model has two per block plus one at the end.
  • Config trace: rms_norm_eps in a Llama, Mistral or Qwen config tells you the norm is RMSNorm (lab L0).

Afterlife

  • GPT-2 moved LayerNorm to the input of each sub-block and added a final norm (deck 02), a year before the theory.
  • LLaMA: "pre-normalization" with RMSNorm on each sub-layer input, followed by Llama 2 and 3, Mistral, Qwen, DeepSeek and gpt-oss.
  • Gemma 2 normalizes both the input and the output of each sub-layer with RMSNorm; Gemma 3 adds QK-norm, normalizing queries and keys inside attention.
  • Lab L6 trains the variants side by side.
