Problem: too many recipes to compare

  • By 2019 there were GPT (decoder, next token), BERT (encoder, masked tokens), and many variants, each with its own data, size, objective and fine-tuning head.
  • Their results could not be compared: every paper changed several things at once.
  • T5 is a controlled study. It fixes one framework and one dataset, then changes one factor at a time: architecture, objective, data, transfer method, scale.

Change: one format for every task

  • A task prefix in plain text replaces task-specific heads; even regression outputs a number as text.
  • One model, one loss (cross-entropy on output tokens), one decoding procedure for every task. Instruction tuning later generalized the prefix into a natural-language instruction.

Change: span corruption

Thank you <X> me to your party <Y> week. → <X> for inviting <Y> last <Z>

  • Corrupt 15% of tokens; replace each consecutive dropped span with one sentinel token. The decoder outputs only the dropped spans, each after its sentinel.
  • Targets are short, so pre-training is cheaper than reconstructing the whole input.
  • Mean span length 3 did slightly better than i.i.d. token corruption on most non-translation tasks.
  • It is BERT's masked LM turned generative: the missing text is produced by a decoder instead of classified per position.
Raffel et al. 2019, arXiv:1910.10683 §3.1.4, §3.3, Figure 2 · bibliography

Architecture details T5 changed

  • Relative position bias. No position embeddings. Each attention logit gets a learned scalar bias that depends on the key-query offset, with 32 buckets that grow logarithmically up to an offset of 128, shared across layers (deck 09 compares ALiBi).
  • Simplified LayerNorm. Rescale only, no additive bias, applied to the sublayer input outside the residual path: pre-norm, close to RMSNorm (deck 17).
  • Data. C4, about 750 GB of cleaned English from one Common Crawl snapshot (April 2019).
  • Vocabulary. 32,000 SentencePiece wordpieces shared by encoder and decoder, trained on English, German, French and Romanian.

BART: a denoising sequence-to-sequence model

  • Bidirectional encoder plus autoregressive decoder, GELU; 6 + 6 layers (base) and 12 + 12 (large, d = 1024).
  • Corrupt a document and train the decoder to rebuild all of it. Noise types: token masking, token deletion, text infilling (Poisson λ = 3 span lengths, each span → one [MASK]), sentence permutation, document rotation.
  • Text infilling worked most consistently. The large model used infilling plus sentence permutation on 30% of tokens.
  • Matched RoBERTa on GLUE and SQuAD and gained about 6 ROUGE-1 on XSum summarization (45.14 vs 38.81).

Modeling impact

  • Where encoder-decoders fit. Input and output are different sequences: translation, summarization, speech to text. Whisper (deck 40) is an encoder-decoder for this reason.
  • Why they lost for LLMs. Two stacks, cross-attention and a split between input and output complicate long open-ended generation and multi-turn chat, where the "input" keeps growing with the output.
  • Text-to-text is the idea that survived most: instruction tuning and chat formats state the task in the input text.
  • Relative bias and pre-norm without bias anticipated ALiBi and RMSNorm.

Afterlife and check yourself

  • T5 checkpoints (and their instruction-tuned versions) remain common as text encoders, for example in diffusion pipelines (deck 38).
  • Large decoder-only LLMs won on open-ended generation and in-context learning (deck 05). A decoder with a bidirectional prefix (T5's prefix LM) sits between the two.
