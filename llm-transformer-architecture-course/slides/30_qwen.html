Qwen2.5 and Qwen3

Alibaba's Qwen team, December 2024 and May 2025. Qwen2.5 is a Llama-style dense family trained on 18T tokens with a long-context recipe. Qwen3 adds QK-Norm, fine-grained MoE models and one model that can both think step by step and answer directly.

Problem: one family for every size and language

  • Qwen releases a whole ladder of sizes (0.5B to 72B in Qwen2.5, 0.6B to 235B in Qwen3) so that the same tokenizer, template and behaviour run on a phone or a cluster.
  • Pressure points in 2024: more and better data, long contexts (128K and beyond) and the cost of serving the largest model.
  • In 2025, reasoning models (deck 31) added a new one: long chains of thought are slow, and not every prompt needs one.

Qwen2.5: long context as a recipe

  • Adjusted base frequency (ABF, deck 10) raises the RoPE base so that distant positions still rotate slowly enough to be told apart.
  • YaRN and Dual Chunk Attention extend further at inference time without more training; Qwen2.5-Turbo was trained up to 1M tokens.
  • Post-training: more than 1M SFT samples, offline DPO on about 150K pairs, then online RL with GRPO.
  • Result: Qwen2.5-72B-Instruct is competitive with Llama-3-405B-Instruct, about 5× its size.

Qwen3: pre-training in three stages

  • 36T tokens in 119 languages and dialects: twice Qwen2.5's 18T tokens, and up from 29 languages.
  • Part of the data is synthetic: text extracted from PDFs and reasoning, math and code data generated by Qwen2.5 models.

Qwen3: thinking and non-thinking in one model

  • Stage 2 alone lifts the 235B model on AIME'24 from 70.1 to 85.1.
  • Stage 3 trains both modes into one model, selected by a flag in the chat template; an empty thinking block marks the non-thinking answer.
  • Thinking budget: when the reasoning reaches a token limit, it is cut off and the model is told to answer now, so the user trades latency for quality.
  • Small models: strong-to-weak distillation from the flagships. For Qwen3-8B, on-policy distillation reached 74.4 on AIME'24 against 67.6 for RL, with 1,800 GPU hours instead of 17,920.

Modeling impact

  • Small details stabilize training. QKV bias in Qwen2.5 and QK-Norm in Qwen3 are both about controlling attention logits; QK-Norm is also used by Gemma 3 and OLMo 2 (deck 32).
  • Fine-grained MoE without shared experts is a live alternative to DeepSeek's shared-plus-routed design (decks 22, 31).
  • Reasoning becomes a mode, not a model. One set of weights serves both quick and step-by-step answers, controlled by the template (deck 11).
  • Distillation is cheaper than RL for small models, which shapes how small open models are now produced (compare Gemma 2, deck 32).
