Problem: MQA is fast but costs quality

  • Multi-query attention (deck 12) cuts decoding cost by sharing one key and value across all heads, but loses some quality.
  • A team with a strong multi-head model does not want to pre-train a second, multi-query model just for fast inference.
  • Two questions: can a multi-head checkpoint be converted cheaply, and is there a point between one KV head and h KV heads that keeps most of the quality and most of the speed?

Change 1: uptrain an existing checkpoint

  • The key and value projection matrices of the heads in a group are averaged into one. Mean pooling worked better than keeping one head or initializing new heads randomly.
  • The converted model then trains for a fraction α of its original steps with the same recipe. α = 5% took about 600 TPUv3 chip-days for T5-XXL.

Change 2: G groups of query heads

GQA-G splits the h query heads into G groups; each group shares one key head and one value head. GQA-1 is MQA and GQA-h is MHA. The KV cache shrinks by h / G.

Evidence: what the ablations show

  • Conversion: mean pooling > keeping the first head > random initialization, in order of how much of the pre-trained model each keeps.
  • Uptraining: GQA is already reasonable right after conversion; MQA needs uptraining. Both gain from 5% and gain little past 10%.
  • Number of groups: going from 1 to 8 groups adds little inference time; cost rises steeply as G approaches the 64 heads of MHA. The authors chose 8 as a "favorable middle ground".

Afterlife

  • Llama 2 used GQA for its 34B and 70B models only; Llama 3 used 8 KV heads at every size.
  • Mistral 7B: 8 KV heads. Gemma 2: num_groups = 2. gpt-oss: 64 query heads and 8 KV heads.
  • In a decoder-only model every attention layer is decoder self-attention, so GQA applies to all of them.
  • Uptraining, converting an architecture with a short continued pre-training, became a reusable trick, used again for context extension (deck 10).
