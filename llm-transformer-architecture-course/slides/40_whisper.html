Problem: models that ace LibriSpeech fail elsewhere

  • Speech recognition models were pre-trained without labels (wav2vec 2.0) and then fine-tuned on a clean dataset such as LibriSpeech.
  • They reached superhuman scores on that dataset but made many more errors on other recordings: they had learned the dataset, not speech.
  • Bet: scale supervised training on diverse, noisy labels instead, and evaluate zero-shot, as GPT-2 did for text (deck 02).

Change: tasks as tokens

<|startoftranscript|> <|en|> <|transcribe|> <|0.00|> So let's begin. <|2.40|> ... <|endoftranscript|>
<|startoftranscript|> <|fr|> <|translate|> <|notimestamps|> Good morning everyone. <|endoftranscript|>
  • A language token (99 languages) or <|nospeech|>, then the task, then optional timestamps quantized to 20 ms and interleaved with the text.
  • One decoder handles language identification, voice activity, transcription, translation and alignment; the prompt tokens pick the task.
  • This is the chat-template idea of deck 11 applied to speech: special tokens turn a sequence model into a multitask model.

Evidence: robustness

  • Compared with a supervised LibriSpeech model of the same clean-test error, the best zero-shot Whisper makes 55.2% fewer errors on average across other speech datasets.
  • On a set of real-world recordings, Whisper's English accuracy is close to professional human transcribers: one computer-assisted service was 1.15 points of WER better, and pure-human transcription only a fraction of a point better.
  • Performance grows with model size and with hours of data per language.

Modeling impact

  • Data diversity beats benchmark fit. Robustness came from 680K hours of messy audio, not from a new architecture.
  • The encoder-decoder survives. For a task with a long input in one modality and a short output in another, the 2017 design (deck 01) is still natural.
  • Audio front ends for LLMs. Whisper's encoder is reused as the audio input of later speech-language models, the way CLIP's encoder is for images (deck 36).
  • Continuous input, discrete output. Whisper reads log-Mel features; generating audio needs discrete audio tokens instead (decks 41–42).
