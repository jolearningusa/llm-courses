AudioLM and VALL-E

Borsos et al. (Google, 2022) and Wang et al. (Microsoft, 2023). Generate audio the way GPT generates text: predict codec tokens one after another. AudioLM continues speech and piano from a few seconds of prompt; VALL-E turns text-to-speech into language modeling and clones a voice from 3 seconds.

AudioLM: three stages

  • Each stage is the same decoder-only Transformer: 12 layers, 16 heads, width 1,024, FFN 4,096, about 0.3B parameters.
  • The semantic stage gives long-term coherence; the acoustic stages add speaker, prosody and recording conditions.
Borsos et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.03143 §III-C, §IV-B · bibliography
3 / 8

AudioLM: evidence

  • From a 3-second prompt of an unseen speaker, a speaker classifier matched the continuation to the prompt's speaker 92.6% of the time.
  • Human raters told real from generated continuations 51.2% of the time, not significantly different from chance.
  • A classifier trained to detect AudioLM speech reached 98.6% accuracy, as a safeguard.
  • Trained on 40K hours of piano, it continues music; raters preferred it over an acoustic-only model 83.3% of the time.
Borsos et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.03143 §IV-F to §IV-I, Table III · bibliography

VALL-E: TTS as conditional language modeling

  • The autoregressive model decides timing and content through the first codebook; the non-autoregressive model fills the finer codebooks, one level per pass. Both: 12 layers, 16 heads, width 1,024, FFN 4,096.
  • Data: LibriLight, about 60K hours of English from more than 7,000 speakers, transcribed by an ASR model.
Wang et al. 2023, arXiv:2301.02111 §1, §3, §4.1–4.2, §5.1 · bibliography

Modeling impact

  • Speech generation becomes language modeling. With codec tokens, the decoder-only Transformer, sampling and prompting all carry over from text.
  • Hierarchy handles the token rate. Coarse-then-fine (AudioLM) and AR-then-NAR (VALL-E) avoid predicting 600 tokens per second one by one.
  • In-context learning for voices. A 3-second prompt plays the role of few-shot examples (deck 05): no fine-tuning per speaker.
  • Misuse risk. Voice cloning from seconds of audio is why both papers discuss detection and responsible release.
