Problem: parameters cost compute

  • In a dense network every parameter is used for every token, so model capacity and compute per token grow together (decks 05–06).
  • Conditional computation, activating only part of the network per input, had been proposed for years, but in practice ran into algorithmic and performance problems.
  • Shazeer et al. list why: GPUs favor arithmetic over branching; selecting parts of a network shrinks the batch each part sees; network bandwidth; and the need for extra losses to keep the selection balanced.

Change: the sparsely-gated MoE layer

y = Σi G(x)i Ei(x) , G(x) = Softmax(KeepTopK(H(x), k))

  • Experts are feed-forward networks. H(x) = xWg plus Gaussian noise scaled by Softplus(xWnoise); entries outside the top k become −∞, so their gates are 0.
  • Placed between stacked LSTM layers, applied at every position.
Shazeer et al. 2017, arXiv:1701.06538 §2, Eqs. 1–5 · bibliography
3 / 10

Keeping the experts busy

  • Balance losses. Without them the router favors a few experts, which then train faster and get chosen more. The paper adds w · CV(Importance)², where importance is each expert's total gate value in the batch, and a second loss on a smooth estimate of the number of tokens each expert receives.
  • The shrinking batch. With n experts and k chosen, each expert sees about kb/n of a batch of b. Combining data parallelism (replicas) with model parallelism (one copy of each expert, fed by all replicas) multiplies each expert's batch by the number of devices d.

GShard: MoE inside the Transformer

  • Expert capacity: each expert takes at most about 2N/(G·E) tokens from a group of N/G tokens; tokens whose experts are full skip the layer through the residual connection.
  • Auxiliary loss: mean over experts of (ce/S) · me, the fraction of tokens sent to expert e times its mean gate value. Random routing: the second expert is used with probability proportional to its gate.

Modeling impact

  • Total and active parameters separate. A model can hold far more parameters than it uses per token. Every MoE report since quotes both numbers.
  • The FFN is the sparse part. Attention stays dense; experts replace feed-forward layers, which hold most parameters (deck 18).
  • Routing is a new failure mode. Balance losses, capacity limits and dropped tokens become hyperparameters.
  • The model is a distributed system. Experts live on different devices, so all-to-all communication and expert parallelism (compute course) are part of the design.

Afterlife

  • Switch Transformer (deck 20) simplified routing to one expert and fixed the training instabilities.
  • Top-2 routing over 8 experts reappears in Mixtral (deck 21).
  • The auxiliary balance loss is the default until DeepSeek replaces most of it with a bias term (deck 22).
  • Noisy gating dropped out; capacity factors and token dropping are still used in training, and inference systems avoid dropping.
