Problem: models fail past their training length

  • Training cost grows with sequence length, so it is tempting to train short and run long.
  • The authors measured extrapolation directly. Sinusoidal embeddings improve only slightly beyond the training length and then degrade. Rotary does better but still falls short.
  • T5's learned relative bias extrapolates better, but trained at least twice as slowly as the sinusoidal model in their setup.
  • Goal: a position method that extrapolates and costs nothing extra to train.

Change: a fixed linear penalty per head

softmax(qi Kᵀ + m · [−(i−1), …, −2, −1, 0])

  • No position embedding anywhere. The only position signal is a bias added to the attention scores, proportional to the distance between query and key.
  • Slope m is fixed, not learned, and different for each head: a geometric sequence. With 8 heads the slopes are 1/2, 1/4, …, 1/256.
  • Steep heads look mostly at recent tokens; shallow heads can still reach far back.

Evidence

  • A 1.3B-parameter model trained on 1,024-token inputs with ALiBi matches the perplexity of a sinusoidal model trained on 2,048 tokens, when both are evaluated at 2,048.
  • The ALiBi model trained about 11% faster and used about 11% less memory.
  • Perplexity keeps improving or stays flat as evaluation length grows well past the training length, where the other methods degrade.
  • The same slopes worked across the text domains and model sizes tested, with no tuning per model.

What the extrapolation actually is

  • The paper's own analysis: much of the gain at longer evaluation lengths comes from the "early token curse". When text is evaluated in non-overlapping chunks, the first tokens of each chunk have little context; longer chunks mean fewer such tokens.
  • With sliding-window evaluation, where every prediction has full context, ALiBi's perplexity stays roughly flat as the input grows. It is not clearly using the extra distant tokens.
  • The authors present this as a hypothesis. It foreshadows "Lost in the Middle" (deck 11): fitting more tokens into the window is not the same as using them.

Modeling impact

  • A recency prior. Every head is pushed toward nearby tokens, which suits language modeling and is part of why it extrapolates gracefully.
  • No parameters, nothing in the residual stream, one bias add per score. FlashAttention-style kernels can fuse it.
  • Long-range retrieval suffers. A fixed linear penalty makes it hard to attend sharply to one token far away, the task long-context models are later judged on.

Afterlife

  • ALiBi was used by several open models of 2022–2023, including BLOOM and MPT.
  • RoPE with context-extension methods (deck 10) became the standard instead: it keeps sharp long-range retrieval and can be stretched after pre-training.
  • The idea of a cheap distance bias returns in sliding-window and local attention (deck 15) and in models that mix RoPE layers with position-free layers (Llama 4, deck 29).
  • ALiBi's evaluation lesson stuck: measure long-context use with tasks that need distant tokens, not only perplexity.
