Problem: recurrence is sequential

  • In 2017 the strongest translation models were RNN or LSTM encoder-decoders with attention. Each step waits for the previous hidden state, so a sentence of n tokens takes n sequential steps in training too.
  • Information between two distant words passes through every state in between. Long-range dependencies are hard to learn.
  • Convolutional models (ByteNet, ConvS2S) parallelize, but the path between distant positions still grows with distance.
  • Goal: a model whose training parallelizes across positions and where any two positions are one step apart.

Change 1: scaled dot-product attention

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q Kᵀ / √dk) V

  • Each query scores every key; the softmax weights mix the values. All positions are computed in one matrix product.
  • Why divide by √dk: with large dk the dot products grow in magnitude and push the softmax into regions with extremely small gradients.
  • A mask sets disallowed scores to −∞ before the softmax. The decoder masks future positions so training stays autoregressive.
Vaswani et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.03762 §3.2.1, §3.2.3 · bibliography

Change 2: many heads in parallel

headi = Attention(QWiQ, KWiK, VWiV) ; MultiHead = Concat(head1..h) WO

  • Base model: h = 8 heads, each with dk = dv = dmodel/h = 64. Total cost is close to one full-width head.
  • Different heads can attend to different positions and relations at once, which one averaged head cannot.
  • Every head has its own K and V. That choice is what MQA (deck 12), GQA (13) and MLA (16) revisit to shrink the KV cache.
Vaswani et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.03762 §3.2.2 · bibliography

The full model

  • Each sublayer is wrapped as LayerNorm(x + Sublayer(x)): a residual connection, then normalization (post-LN).
  • FFN(x) = max(0, xW₁ + b₁)W₂ + b₂, applied to each position, with dff = 2048 = 4 × dmodel.
  • Cross-attention takes queries from the decoder and keys and values from the encoder memory.

Change 3: positions without recurrence

PE(pos, 2i) = sin(pos / 100002i/d) ; PE(pos, 2i+1) = cos(pos / 100002i/d)

  • Attention alone is order-blind: permuting the input permutes the output. Position must be injected.
  • The sinusoids are added to the token embeddings. For a fixed offset k, PE(pos + k) is a linear function of PE(pos), which the authors hoped would let attention use relative position.
  • Learned position embeddings gave nearly identical results (Table 3, row E). The sinusoid was kept because it might extrapolate to longer sequences.
  • Deck 08 shows how RoPE turns the same frequencies into a rotation of queries and keys.

Modeling impact

  • Parallel training. Removing recurrence made it practical to train on far more data. Every scaling result in decks 05 and 06 depends on this.
  • One block, many uses. The encoder and decoder stacks became separate families: encoder-only BERT (deck 03), decoder-only GPT (02), and encoder-decoder T5 (04).
  • A new memory cost. At inference the decoder keeps K and V for every past token and layer: the KV cache. Its size, 2 × layers × heads × dk per token, drives Part III.
  • A quadratic term. Attention compute and score memory grow as n², which only mattered once contexts grew long.
