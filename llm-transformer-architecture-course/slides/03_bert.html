Problem: a left-to-right model sees half the sentence

  • GPT-1 pre-trains left to right, so the representation of each token depends only on what comes before it.
  • Understanding tasks (question answering, entailment, tagging) need context from both sides of a word.
  • A bidirectional model cannot be trained with the plain next-token loss: each word would see itself through the layers above.
  • ELMo combined two separate one-way LSTMs. BERT asks for one deep model that is bidirectional in every layer.
Devlin et al. 2018, arXiv:1810.04805 §1–2 · bibliography
2 / 10

Change: masked language modeling

  • Pick 15% of WordPiece tokens at random and predict the original token at those positions from the full, unmasked context.
  • Of the chosen tokens, 80% become [MASK], 10% a random token, 10% stay unchanged. This reduces the mismatch with fine-tuning, where [MASK] never appears.
  • Plus next sentence prediction (NSP): given sentences A and B, predict whether B followed A (50% of the time) or was random.
  • No causal mask: the attention mask only hides padding.

Cost of the trick: only 15% of positions give a training signal per pass, against 100% for a causal LM.

Evidence

  • New state of the art on eleven NLP tasks. GLUE 80.5 on the leaderboard (+7.7 points), MultiNLI 86.7%, SQuAD v1.1 test F1 93.2, SQuAD v2.0 test F1 83.1.
  • Ablation on BERT-base dev sets: a left-to-right model without NSP drops SQuAD F1 from 88.5 to 77.8 and MRPC from 86.7 to 77.5. Bidirectionality is the main gain.
  • Removing NSP lowered QNLI from 88.4 to 84.9 and MNLI-m from 84.4 to 83.9 in BERT's own setup.
  • Larger models helped even on small datasets such as MRPC (3,600 training examples).

Modeling impact

  • Encoder-only is the right shape for representation. Every token sees the whole input, so per-token and per-sequence outputs are strong for classification, tagging, retrieval and reranking.
  • It cannot generate. There is no causal order, so producing text needs many passes or a separate decoder. This is why chat models are decoders.
  • Sample efficiency. Masked LM learns from 15% of positions per pass; a causal LM learns from all of them. At large scale this favored decoders (deck 04 measures it).
  • Fixed length. Learned positions cap inputs at 512 tokens.

Afterlife

  • Encoder-only models remain the default for embedding, retrieval and classification, where their bidirectional context and small size pay off.
  • The masked objective returns in vision as masked patch prediction and in T5's span corruption (deck 04), which keeps the idea but generates the missing spans with a decoder.
  • RoBERTa's lesson, that a fixed architecture can be badly undertrained, is the same lesson Chinchilla (deck 06) draws for decoders.
  • Segment embeddings and NSP disappeared; [CLS]/[SEP]-style special tokens survived and grew into chat templates (deck 11).
