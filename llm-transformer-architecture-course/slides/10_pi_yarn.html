Problem: pre-training long is expensive

  • Attention cost grows with length (deck 01), so most pre-training runs at 2K–8K tokens. Applications want 32K–128K.
  • Running a RoPE model past its trained window feeds rotation angles it has never seen. Perplexity explodes.
  • Fine-tuning directly on longer sequences adapts slowly: in the PI paper, LLaMA's effective window only grew from 2,048 to about 2,560 after more than 10,000 steps.

Change 1: Position Interpolation (PI)

f′(x, m) = f(x, m · L / L′)

  • Squeeze the new window L′ into the old one L by scaling every position down. All angles stay inside the trained range.
  • The paper bounds the attention-score error: the interpolation bound is at least about 600× smaller than the extrapolation bound in the LLaMA 7B setting.
Chen et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.15595 §2 · bibliography

PI evidence

  • LLaMA 7B to 65B extended from 2,048 to as many as 32,768 tokens with about 1,000 fine-tuning steps on the Pile.
  • Passkey retrieval reached the full new window within about 200 steps.
  • Perplexity on long documents (PG-19, Proof-pile) improved as the window grew.
  • Cost: mild regressions inside the original window, up to about 2% on benchmarks at 8,192 tokens and more for larger windows. Squeezing positions blurs fine local order.
Chen et al. 2023, arXiv:2306.15595 §3 · bibliography

Change 2: YaRN treats frequencies differently

  • Diagnosis. PI scales every RoPE pair equally. Fast-rotating pairs, whose wavelength is much shorter than the window, carry local order and do not need scaling; scaling them is what blurs short-range detail.
  • NTK-aware: change the RoPE base instead of the positions, so high frequencies change little and low frequencies a lot.
  • NTK-by-parts: for each pair, compare its wavelength λ with the context L (r = L/λ). Fully interpolate pairs with small r, leave pairs with large r unchanged, blend in between (α = 1, β = 32 for Llama).
  • Attention temperature: scale logits with 1/√t = 0.1 ln(s) + 1, where s is the extension factor. It folds into the RoPE tables at no runtime cost.
Peng et al. 2023, arXiv:2309.00071 §3 · bibliography

YaRN evidence

  • Llama 2 7B and 13B extended to 64K with scale s = 16; a further 200 steps at s = 32 reach 128K, with over 99% passkey accuracy at 128K.
  • The authors report 10× fewer tokens and 2.5× fewer training steps than previous methods.
  • Dynamic scaling at inference sets s = max(1, l′/L) from the current length, extending a model more than 2× with no fine-tuning. Keys must then be cached before RoPE is applied, since the rotation changes with s.
Peng et al. 2023, arXiv:2309.00071 §3–4 · bibliography

Modeling impact

  • Context length became a post-training knob. Pre-train short and cheap, then extend in a short final stage. Llama 3 grew from 8K to 128K in six stages near the end of pre-training.
  • The KV cache grows with the window. A 128K window at Llama 3 8B's 128 KiB per token is 16 GiB per sequence (lab L0), which is what Part III works to shrink.
  • Window ≠ use. Passing passkey retrieval shows a model can find one fact; it does not show it uses the whole window (deck 11).

Afterlife

  • Released configs carry the choice in rope_theta and rope_scaling. Llama 3 8B uses θ = 500,000 with no scaling; Qwen2.5 uses θ = 1,000,000 and documents YaRN for inputs beyond 32K.
  • Raising the base (the NTK-aware idea in its simplest form) became the default first step; YaRN-style per-frequency scaling the default second step.
  • Sliding windows (deck 15), compressed KV (deck 16) and sparse attention (deck 26) attack the cost of long windows rather than the positions.
