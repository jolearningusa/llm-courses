Problem: absolute or relative?

  • Absolute embeddings (sinusoidal in deck 01, learned in GPT and BERT) are added to the input. What attention needs is usually relative: "the word two to the left".
  • Relative schemes (Shaw et al., T5's bias in deck 04) add terms inside attention. They work, but change the attention computation and do not fit linear-attention variants.
  • Learned absolute tables also fix a maximum length.
  • Goal: an encoding applied to q and k such that qm·kn is a function of the content and of m − n only.

Change: rotate each pair of dimensions

f(x, m) = RΘ,m W x ; qmᵀ kn = xᵀ Wqᵀ RΘ,n−m Wk xn

  • Split the d dimensions of q and k into d/2 pairs. Rotate pair i by angle m·θi, with θi = 10000−2(i−1)/d: the same frequencies as the 2017 sinusoids.
  • A rotation by m followed by the inverse of a rotation by n is a rotation by n − m, so the dot product sees only the offset.
  • Applied to queries and keys in every layer, not to values and not to the input embedding.

Same offset, same score

Moving both tokens by the same amount rotates both vectors by the same angle, so their dot product is unchanged. Lab L3 checks this numerically.

Implementation and properties

RoPE(x)2i = x2i cos mθi − x2i+1 sin mθi ; RoPE(x)2i+1 = x2i sin mθi + x2i+1 cos mθi

  • Element-wise with precomputed cos and sin tables: no extra parameters and negligible compute.
  • Properties claimed by the paper: no fixed maximum length; a long-term decay of the dot-product bound with distance; compatible with linear attention because rotations preserve norms.
  • High-frequency pairs turn fast and encode fine local order; low-frequency pairs turn slowly and encode coarse position. Deck 10 extends context by treating the two ends differently.

Evidence in the paper

  • WMT14 En→De: 27.5 BLEU against 27.3 for the Transformer baseline.
  • BERT-style pre-training: RoFormer's masked-LM loss converged faster than BERT's.
  • CAIL2019-SCM (long Chinese legal documents): 68.29% at 512 tokens against 67.77% for BERT; 69.79% when RoFormer used 1,024 tokens.
  • The authors say plainly that they lack a full explanation of why it converges faster. The adoption came less from these numbers than from the simplicity and the relative property.

Modeling impact

  • Relative position at no cost. No table to learn, no bias term, nothing added to the residual stream.
  • KV cache stores rotated keys. Each key is rotated once by its own position and cached; new queries are rotated by theirs. MLA (deck 16) has to work around this when it compresses keys.
  • Context length becomes a frequency question. Positions past training produce rotation angles the model never saw on its slowest pairs. Raising the base θ (Llama 3: 500,000) or rescaling positions (deck 10) is how models reach 128K.
