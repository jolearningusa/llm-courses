Problem: does diffusion need a U-Net?

  • DDPM and latent diffusion (deck 38) used convolutional U-Nets, with attention only at a few resolutions.
  • Language and vision (decks 05, 34) had shown that Transformers scale predictably with compute. Diffusion had no such study.
  • Question: is the U-Net's inductive bias needed, or does a standard Transformer do as well and scale better?

Change: patchify the latent

  • DiT works in the latent space of Stable Diffusion's off-the-shelf VAE (deck 38).
  • Patch size p ∈ {2, 4, 8}. Halving p multiplies the tokens by 4, and the Gflops by at least 4, with almost no change in parameters (compare ViT, deck 34).

Evidence: Gflops predict quality

  • Across the 12 size and patch combinations, FID falls steadily with model Gflops, whether the extra compute comes from depth, width or more tokens.
  • Models with similar Gflops reach similar FID: DiT-S/2 and DiT-B/4 are close.
  • Parameters alone do not predict quality: a smaller patch adds compute but almost no parameters, and it helps.

Modeling impact

  • Diffusion joins the Transformer scaling story. Quality becomes a function of compute, so the tools of decks 05–06 apply.
  • The backbone for large generators. Later image and video generators (Stable Diffusion 3, Sora as described by OpenAI) use Transformer backbones over latent patches.
  • Conditioning by modulation. adaLN-Zero shows that a global signal (time, class) can enter by scaling and shifting norms instead of extra tokens or attention.
  • Tokens as the compute knob. Patch size trades compute for quality without changing parameters, the same trade that ViT and VLM resamplers make.
