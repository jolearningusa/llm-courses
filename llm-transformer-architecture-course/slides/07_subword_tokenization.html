Problem: words are an open set

  • 2015 translation models used a fixed vocabulary of 30,000 to 50,000 words. Everything else became an unknown token or went through a back-off dictionary.
  • Names, compounds (German Abwasserbehandlungsanlage), numbers and morphology make the long tail endless, worst in agglutinative languages.
  • Characters solve coverage but make sequences several times longer, and attention cost grows with length (deck 01).
  • Goal: a fixed-size vocabulary that can still spell any word, with frequent words as one token.

Change 1: byte-pair encoding

  1. Start from characters, with an end-of-word marker; count words in the corpus.
  2. Count every adjacent symbol pair, weighted by word frequency. Merge the most frequent pair into a new symbol.
  3. Repeat. The number of merges is the only hyperparameter, and it sets the vocabulary size.
  4. To encode new text, split into characters and replay the merges in order. Unseen words still segment into known pieces.

l o w e s t → lo w e s t → low e s t → low est

Up to +1.1 BLEU (En→De) and +1.3 BLEU (En→Ru) over a back-off dictionary on WMT15, with better translation of rare and unseen words.

Change 2: the unigram language model

  • Start from a large seed vocabulary of characters and frequent substrings. Fit a probability p(x) to each piece with EM.
  • A segmentation's probability is the product of its pieces' probabilities; the best one is found with Viterbi.
  • Prune: drop the pieces whose removal costs the least likelihood, keep the top 80% each round (always keeping single characters), until the target size.
  • Subword regularization: during training, sample a different segmentation each time, with probability ∝ P(x)α. On WMT14 En→De, 25.04 BLEU vs 24.53 for BPE.

BPE builds up by merging; Unigram trims down by likelihood. Both give a fixed vocabulary of subword pieces.

Modeling impact

  • Context length is counted in tokens. A 128K window holds about 500K English characters at 3.94 characters per token. A language the tokenizer serves poorly gets less text into the same window and pays more per word.
  • Parameters. Vocabulary × dmodel in the embedding, and again in an untied output head. For Llama 3 8B that is 2 × 128,256 × 4,096 ≈ 1.05B parameters, 13% of the model (lab L0).
  • Softmax cost. Every decoding step computes a logit for every vocabulary entry.
  • What the model can see. Digits, spelling and whitespace are only visible as tokenized. Arithmetic and character-level tasks inherit those splits.

  • Byte-level BPE, or SentencePiece with byte fallback, is the default: no input can produce an unknown token.
  • Vocabularies grew from 32K to 128K–262K as models became multilingual and as large embedding tables became cheap relative to the rest of the model.
  • Special tokens (begin of text, end of turn, role headers, tool calls) are added to the vocabulary and never produced by merges. Deck 11 builds chat templates out of them.
  • Multimodal models extend the idea: image patches (deck 34) and audio codec codes (deck 41) become tokens too.
