Multi-query attention (MQA)

Shazeer, 2019: "Fast Transformer Decoding: One Write-Head is All You Need". Keep many query heads but give them one shared key and one shared value, so the cache that decoding re-reads at every step shrinks by the number of heads.

Problem: decoding is limited by memory, not arithmetic

  • Training processes all positions in parallel. Generation produces one token at a time, and each step re-reads the cached keys and values of every earlier token, in every layer.
  • Shazeer counts memory traffic against arithmetic for one decoding step of multi-head attention: the ratio is Θ(n/d + 1/b), for n cached tokens, model width d and batch b.
  • When the context n approaches d, or the batch is small, the ratio nears 1 and the step waits on memory bandwidth. The cache, not the weights, is the term that grows with context.

Change: one key head and one value head

  • Queries keep h heads. Keys and values are projected once (width dk) and shared by every head.
  • The memory-to-arithmetic ratio becomes Θ(1/d + n/(dh) + 1/b): the n/d term falls by the factor h.

Modeling impact

  • The cache becomes a design target. Before MQA, attention was judged on quality and training FLOPs. After it, KV bytes per token is a first-class number on every architecture (Part III, lab L0).
  • Parameters move. K and V projections shrink from d×d to d×dk each; the paper spent the saving on a wider FFN.
  • The cost is a small quality drop, and models must be trained with MQA from the start. Deck 13 removes both limits.
  • The idea is independent of the rest of the block: it composes with RoPE (deck 08), sliding windows (deck 15) and MoE (Part V).

Afterlife

  • PaLM (2022) and Gemma 2B used multi-query attention. Gemma's 2B checkpoint has 8 query heads and 1 KV head; its 7B checkpoint kept multi-head attention.
  • Most later open models chose the middle ground of deck 13: Llama 2 34B and 70B, Llama 3, Mistral 7B and Qwen2.5 all use grouped-query attention with several KV heads.
  • DeepSeek went the other way and compressed the cache instead of sharing it (MLA, deck 16).
