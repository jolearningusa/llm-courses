Llama 3 and Llama 4

Meta, July 2024 and April 2025. Llama 3 keeps the dense Llama block and scales data (15T tokens), vocabulary (128K) and context (128K). Llama 4 is the first Llama built from mixture-of-experts layers and the first trained on text and images together.

Problem: three levers, chosen for stability

  • The Llama 3 report names three levers for a foundation model: data, scale and managing complexity.
  • Data: about 15T multilingual tokens, against 1.8T for Llama 2.
  • Scale: 3.8 × 1025 FLOPs for the flagship, almost 50× the largest Llama 2.
  • Complexity: a standard dense Transformer with minor adaptations, chosen over mixture-of-experts "to maximize training stability", and SFT, rejection sampling and DPO rather than more complex RL.

Why 405B: a scaling law on the downstream task

  • Meta fit scaling laws with small models (budgets from 6 × 1018 to 1022 FLOPs) and then mapped loss to benchmark accuracy.
  • For the 3.8 × 1025 budget, the fit pointed to about 402B parameters on 16.55T tokens. They trained 405B.
  • The 8B and 70B models were trained much longer than compute-optimal, for the reason in deck 28: they are cheaper to serve.
  • Hardware: up to 16K H100 GPUs. Context was raised from 8K to 128K in six stages, using about 800B tokens.

Llama 4: what else changed

  • Layers alternate. Maverick alternates dense and MoE layers.
  • Early fusion. Text and vision tokens enter the same backbone from the start of pre-training, instead of an adapter added later (deck 37).
  • iRoPE. Some attention layers use no positional embedding at all, interleaved with RoPE layers, and attention temperature is scaled at inference time; Scout is advertised with a 10M-token context after pre- and post-training at 256K.
  • Training. More than 30T tokens over 200 languages, FP8 for Behemoth, and MetaP for setting per-layer learning rates and initialization scales.

Llama 4 has a blog post and model cards but no technical report; treat these claims as the vendor's.

Modeling impact

  • Dense is a deliberate choice. In 2024 Meta chose a dense model to reduce risk at 16K GPUs; by 2025 it switched to MoE, as DeepSeek and Qwen had (decks 30–31).
  • Vocabulary is a lever. 128K tokens cut the sequence length for the same text, which lowers cost per document (deck 07).
  • Long context is a training stage. A raised RoPE base and staged context growth replaced inference-only tricks (deck 10).
  • Positions are optional in some layers. iRoPE is an example of mixing position-free and RoPE layers for length generalization (decks 08, 15).
