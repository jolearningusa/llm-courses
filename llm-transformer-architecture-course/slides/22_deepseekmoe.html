Problem: coarse experts mix and duplicate knowledge

  • Knowledge hybridity. With 8 or 16 experts, each expert receives very different tokens and must hold knowledge that is hard to combine in one FFN.
  • Knowledge redundancy. Common knowledge (grammar, frequent facts) is needed by every expert, so each one learns its own copy.
  • Both waste parameters: the experts are less specialized than the MoE idea promises.

Change 1: fine-grained expert segmentation

Top-2 of 16 experts allows C(16, 2) = 120 combinations per token. Splitting each into 4 (top-8 of 64) allows C(64, 8) = 4,426,165,368.

Change 2: shared expert isolation

  • Shared experts hold the common knowledge once, so routed experts can specialize.
  • Total activated experts stay mK: Ks shared plus mK − Ks routed.
  • Expert-level and device-level balance losses (same fi · Pi form as deck 20) keep routing from collapsing and devices evenly loaded.

Problem: the balance loss fights the model

  • An auxiliary balance loss adds gradients unrelated to language modeling. With a small coefficient α, load is unbalanced and routing can collapse; with a large one, balance is good but the model gets worse.
  • Expert-choice routing (experts pick tokens) balances perfectly, but in a decoder it lets a token's routing depend on later tokens in the batch: information leaks from the future.
  • Wang et al. want balance without a loss term and without leaking.
Wang et al. 2024, arXiv:2408.15664 §2.2, §5.2 · bibliography
6 / 10

Change 3: loss-free balancing with a bias

top-K by si,t + bi ; gate gi,t from si,t only ; bi ← bi + u · sign(load error)

  • Each expert has a bias bi added to its routing score only when choosing the top K. The weight applied to the expert's output ignores the bias.
  • After each batch, raise the bias of experts that got fewer tokens than average and lower it for those that got more, by a fixed step u = 0.001.
  • The bias uses past batches only, so no future token affects routing. No gradient, no interference.
Wang et al. 2024, arXiv:2408.15664 §3, Algorithm 1, §4.3 · bibliography

Modeling impact and afterlife

  • Many small experts plus shared ones became the dominant MoE layout. DeepSeek-V3: 1 shared and 256 routed experts, 8 active, 671B total and 37B active, balanced by the bias method with a small complementary sequence-wise loss.
  • Kimi K2: 384 experts, 8 active, 1 shared. Qwen3 MoE: 128 experts, 8 active, and no shared experts.
  • Balance became a control problem. A bias nudged by load counts replaced a tuned loss weight.
  • Many small experts mean more, smaller matrix multiplies and more routing traffic, which the compute course treats.
