Problem: dense quality costs dense inference

  • In 2023 the strongest open models were dense: Llama 2 70B used all 70B parameters for every token.
  • Earlier MoE results (decks 19–20) were mostly encoder-decoder models, trained in-house, and not released.
  • Question: does a decoder-only MoE with open weights give 70B-class quality at the per-token cost of a model several times smaller?

Evidence: what the router learned

  • The authors looked for experts that specialize by topic (ArXiv papers, biology, philosophy, math, code) and found "no obvious patterns", with DM Mathematics only marginally different.
  • The router looks syntactic: tokens such as "self" in Python and "Question" in English often go to the same expert, as do indentation tokens in code.
  • Consecutive tokens often share an expert: in layers 15 and 31 the first choice repeats for 22–28% of consecutive tokens, against 12.5% by chance.

Modeling impact

  • Compute and memory part ways. Per token, Mixtral costs about as much as a 13B dense model; in memory it is a 47B model, and all experts must be loaded.
  • Batching changes. In a batch, different tokens pick different experts, so most experts are read every step; the cost advantage is largest in compute-bound settings.
  • Experts are not topics. The idea that each expert is a domain specialist did not hold; specialization is lexical and syntactic.
  • Locality helps serving. Repeated expert choices for consecutive tokens matter for expert caching and expert parallelism (compute course).
Jiang et al. 2024, arXiv:2401.04088 §2.1, §5 · bibliography

Afterlife

  • Mixtral 8x22B followed with the same layout. The "8 experts, top-2" shape was copied widely in 2024.
  • DeepSeekMoE (deck 22) argued that 8 large experts are too coarse and moved to many small experts plus shared ones; Qwen3, DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2 and gpt-oss all use more, smaller experts (Part VII).
  • Mistral's GQA, RoPE and SwiGLU block (decks 08, 13, 18) carried over unchanged; only the FFN changed.
