Long-context continued pre-training

Training on 128K-token sequences from the start would be slow, and long documents are rare. So models are pre-trained short and extended at the end. Llama 2 Long, Fu et al. and ProLong measured what that extension needs: the position encoding, the data, the length and the evaluation.

Problem: long sequences are expensive and rare

  • Attention cost grows with the square of the length, and activation memory grows with it too (deck 09).
  • Most web documents are short; books and code repositories are the main long sources.
  • A model trained at 4K or 8K does not work at 32K: rotary positions it never saw behave unpredictably. Architecture deck 10 covers Position Interpolation and YaRN; this deck covers the training that follows.
  • The test is hard too: perplexity and a single "needle" fact can look fine while real long tasks fail.

Llama 2 Long: a larger RoPE base and 400B tokens

  • Continue pre-training Llama 2 for 400B tokens at 32,768 tokens (7B, 13B) or 16,384 (34B, 70B).
  • RoPE ABF: raise the base frequency from 10,000 to 500,000 so distant tokens decay less. Of the variants tested, it was the only one that kept its retrieval accuracy to 32,768 tokens.
  • Data: "having abundant long texts in the pretrain dataset is not the key." Removing long documents kept most of the gain; quality mattered more than the length distribution.
  • Cost: switching from 4K to 32K after 80% of training reached the same quality as 32K throughout with about 40% fewer FLOPs.
  • The 70B chat model scored 37.7 on ZeroSCROLLS against 36.7 for gpt-3.5-turbo-16k; MMLU stayed at 71.7.

Fu et al.: a little data, the same mixture

  • Hypothesis: using information anywhere in the context is "mostly already acquired" in pre-training; 0.5B to 5B tokens of continued training unlock it at 128K.
  • Per-source upsampling: keep each domain's share of the original mixture and upsample long documents inside each domain. Upsampling only books helped book loss and hurt code loss, and the reverse for code.
  • Loss misleads: two recipes with similar loss had very different retrieval.
  • LLaMA-2 7B at 80K and 13B at 64K, 5B tokens, constant learning rate 2 × 10−5, 4M-token batches: about 5 days on 8 A100s for the 7B model.

ProLong: evaluate after SFT, train longer than you test

  • Evaluation: downstream long tasks (HELMET: recall, retrieval-augmented QA, re-ranking, in-context learning, long QA, summarization), measured after SFT, not perplexity or needles alone.
  • Data: code repositories and books as long sources, mixed with high-quality short data; ablations favored about 60% long and 40% short. Final: 30% code repositories, 30% books, 3% textbooks, 37% short mix.
  • Length: training at 512K improved results measured at 64K.
  • SFT: short instruction data (UltraChat) was enough; even 1% synthetic long instructions hurt.
  • ProLong-8B: from Llama-3-8B-Instruct, 20B tokens at 64K (RoPE base 8 × 106) then 20B at 512K (1.28 × 108).

Training impact and afterlife

  • ProLong-8B averaged 49.4 on HELMET at 128K against 46.5 for Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, with about 5% of its long-context data (40B against 800B tokens); it was behind only on summarization.
  • Llama 3 grew the window from 8K to 128K in six stages over about 800B tokens, with RoPE base 500,000. A stage ended when short-context scores had fully recovered and needle tests passed at that length. Post-training mixed in 0.1% synthetic long-context data.
  • The shared lessons: raise the RoPE base, keep the data mixture and quality, upsample long documents within it, spend a few percent of pre-training on it at the end, and judge by downstream tasks after SFT.
