Training stability: loss spikes, z-loss and QK-norm

Large runs fail in ways small runs rarely show: the loss jumps, the gradient norm explodes, and days of compute are lost. Three reports on what causes it and what each fix does: PaLM, Wortsman et al., and OLMo 2.

Problem: spikes at 540B parameters

  • PaLM 540B saw the loss spike about 20 times during training, with gradient clipping on.
  • The fix was operational: restart from a checkpoint about 100 steps before the spike and skip about 200 to 500 data batches.
  • The authors do not blame bad data: the batches around a spike, replayed from a different, earlier checkpoint, caused no spike. They conclude that spikes come from specific batches meeting a particular model state.
  • Every restart costs wall-clock time and compute, and needs someone watching the loss curve.

Method: z-loss

  • The softmax over the vocabulary is pi = ezi / Z, with Z = Σj ezj.
  • Cross-entropy depends only on differences between logits, so adding the same constant to every logit changes nothing: the logits can drift together without bound.
  • z-loss adds 10−4 · (log Z)2 per token, pulling log Z toward 0. The predicted distribution is unchanged; the logits stay in a range where low-precision arithmetic is safe.
  • PaLM: "increases the stability of training".

Method: reproduce instability on a small model

  • Wortsman et al. show that two large-scale instabilities appear in small models trained with high learning rates, so they can be studied cheaply. Models range from about 2.4M to 4.8B parameters.
InstabilityWhat happensMitigation
Attention logit growthQuery-key logits grow large and attention collapses to one-hotQK-layernorm: normalize queries and keys before the dot product
Output logit divergenceOutput logits drift very negativez-loss, as in PaLM

Learning-rate sensitivity: sweep the learning rate over three orders of magnitude and measure the expected gap between each run's final loss and the best one. A stable recipe has a low score.

Training impact

  • Fewer restarts. PaLM handled spikes by hand; OLMo 2 removes their causes in data, initialization, norms and the optimizer, and measures the result with a spike score.
  • Stability is cross-cutting. The fixes live in the data pipeline (repeated n-grams), the architecture (norm placement, QK-norm), the loss (z-loss) and the optimizer (ε, weight decay).
  • Cheap proxies. Because the failures reproduce at small scale with high learning rates, a recipe can be checked with a learning-rate sweep before the expensive run.
  • The gradient norm is watched because its spikes often come before loss spikes.

Afterlife

  • QK-norm is now part of the block in several families: Qwen3 (architecture deck 30) adds it, and Gemma 3 (deck 32) replaces Gemma 2's logit soft-capping with it.
  • Where the norm goes is an architecture choice with a training motive: deck 17 explains why Pre-LN needs no warm-up.
  • z-loss is kept in OLMo 2. Its weight is given as 10−4 in §3.3.3 and 10−5 in Table 1; check the released config before reusing it.
  • Optimizers bring their own failure modes: Kimi K2 rescales a head's query and key weights whenever its attention logits pass a threshold (QK-Clip), to train with Muon at 1T parameters (deck 04, architecture deck 33).
