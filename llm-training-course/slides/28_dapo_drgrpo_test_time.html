Fixing GRPO and spending test-time compute: DAPO, Dr. GRPO, Snell et al.

Replicating R1-Zero exposed problems in GRPO: entropy collapse, wasted batches, and normalizations that bias answer length. DAPO (2025) fixed four of them and matched R1-Zero-Qwen-32B in half the steps. Dr. GRPO (2025) removed two biased terms and asked how much of the "aha moment" the base model already had. Snell et al. (2024) asked how best to spend compute at inference.

Problem: naive GRPO falls short

  • On Qwen2.5-32B, plain GRPO reached about 30 on AIME 2024; DeepSeek reported 47 for its R1-Zero recipe on the same base (R1-Zero-Qwen-32B: G = 16, KL 0.001, 32K tokens).
  • The policy's entropy collapsed early, so it stopped exploring.
  • Groups whose answers were all right or all wrong had zero advantage and gave no gradient, so effective batches shrank.
  • Long answers were weighted strangely, and truncated answers were punished as if they were wrong.
Yu et al. 2025 (DAPO), arXiv:2503.14476 §1, §3 · DeepSeek-AI 2025 (DeepSeek-R1), arXiv:2501.12948 App. B.4.1 · bibliography

DAPO: four changes

ChangeWhat it does
Clip-Higherεlow = 0.2, εhigh = 0.28: unlikely tokens can grow more, which keeps entropy up
Dynamic samplingDrop groups with accuracy 0 or 1 and sample more until the batch is full
Token-level lossAverage over all tokens in the batch, not per answer, so long answers count fully
Overlong shapingMask truncated answers; then a graded length penalty over a 4,096-token zone near the 16,384-token expected maximum

Also: no KL penalty, since a long-CoT model is meant to move far from its start, and a reward of +1 or −1 from a rule.

Dr. GRPO: two biases in the objective

  • Length bias: dividing each answer's loss by its length |oi| makes short correct answers get larger updates and long wrong answers smaller penalties per token, so wrong answers drift longer.
  • Difficulty bias: dividing by the group's reward std gives extra weight to questions that are nearly always right or nearly always wrong.
  • Fix: remove both; use one constant (the generation budget) in place of |oi|. The advantage is then RLOO's up to a constant (deck 26).
  • Response length stops growing without bound, and wrong answers get shorter, at the same accuracy.
Liu et al. 2025 (Dr. GRPO), arXiv:2503.20783 §3, Eq. 3, Fig. 5, App. A · Lab L7 · bibliography

Dr. GRPO: what the base model already had

  • Templates matter: Qwen2.5 base models answered best with no template, and scored about 60% higher on average than with 4-shot prompting, a hint that question-answer text was in their pre-training data.
  • Self-reflection exists before RL in base models, DeepSeek-V3-Base included; more reflection did not reliably mean higher accuracy.
  • Templates set the starting point; with a suitable question set, RL brought them to about the same accuracy.
  • Result: Oat-Zero-7B reached 43.3% on AIME 2024 from a 7B base, with about 27 hours on 8 A100s.

Test-time compute: search or revise

  • Search against a PRM: best-of-N weighted by the verifier, beam search over steps, or lookahead. Beam search won at small budgets, best-of-N at large ones and on easy questions.
  • Revise: a model fine-tuned to revise its own earlier answers, sequentially. Easy questions did best with all-sequential revisions; hard ones with a mix, such as √N parallel chains of √N revisions.
  • Compute-optimal: choosing the method by estimated question difficulty beat best-of-N with over 4× less compute (PaLM 2-S*, MATH).
  • Against a bigger model at equal FLOPs: test-time compute on a small model beat a ~14× larger model on easy and medium questions; on the hardest, or with much more inference than pre-training, more pre-training won.

Measured reward gains need a separate task oracle

More positive reward events did not establish generalization. For 1+2, the width proxy gives 9<EOS> half credit; the strict evaluator gives zero.

Starting success must be measured: 40 SFT prompts did not ensure a stronger prior. L7: all seeds, ranges, costs and limits. No claim that sparse RL or shaping universally wins.

Training-time steering: poor toy generalization

Same rollout count does not mean the same total work. Each run samples 3,840 training responses; replay and bank fitting add work.

Frozen final checkpoints · 27 held-out operand pairs × 8 samples · steering OFF at evaluation. Learned steering does not win every seed; no reliable ranking.
