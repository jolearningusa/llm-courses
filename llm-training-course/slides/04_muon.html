Muon: orthogonalized updates for hidden matrices

Keller Jordan, 2024, and Moonshot AI's Moonlight report, 2025. Take the momentum of a weight matrix's gradient, replace it by the nearest matrix whose singular values are all about 1, and use that as the update. At scale, it matched AdamW with about half the training FLOPs.

Problem: updates dominated by a few directions

  • AdamW treats every element of a weight matrix on its own; it does not use the fact that the parameter is a matrix acting on vectors.
  • Jordan observes that SGD-momentum and Adam updates for 2-D Transformer weights have a very high condition number: they are nearly low-rank, dominated by a few directions.
  • His hypothesis, stated as speculation: orthogonalizing the update boosts rare directions that have small magnitude but still matter for learning.
  • AdamW also keeps two state buffers per weight (first and second moments) on top of the weights.

Method: momentum, then Newton-Schulz

  1. Momentum on the gradient of each hidden 2-D matrix (Nesterov-style recommended).
  2. Normalize it by its Frobenius norm, then apply five steps of X ← aX + b(XXT)X + c(XXT)2X with (a, b, c) = (3.4445, −4.7750, 2.0315). The result keeps the singular vectors and pushes each singular value toward 1.
  3. Apply it as the update.
  • Only for hidden matrices; Q, K and V are best treated as separate matrices.
  • Embeddings, the output head, and scalar and vector parameters (norm gains, biases) still use AdamW.
  • Extra cost: at most T·m/B of the forward-backward FLOPs, for T steps, width m and B tokens per batch; about 0.7% for the NanoGPT speedrun and 0.5% at Llama 405B's width and batch (lab L0).

What the five steps do

Each step applies the odd polynomial ax + bx3 + cx5 to every singular value. The coefficients trade exactness for speed: they are tuned so that values land in about [0.7, 1.3] instead of exactly at 1; an error of about 0.3 did not harm the loss curve in Jordan's runs. Lab L1 checks this on a matrix with condition number 100.

Method at scale: two changes in Moonlight

  • Weight decay. Add AdamW's decoupled weight decay to Muon. Without it, the RMS of weights and layer outputs kept growing at scale, past the high-precision range of BF16.
  • Match AdamW's update size. An orthogonal update for an A × B matrix has an RMS that depends on its shape. Scale it by 0.2 · √max(A, B), which brings its RMS to about 0.2; AdamW's update RMS is usually 0.2 to 0.4. Unscaled, large matrices such as the MLP get updates that are too small and small ones such as per-head KV projections too large. Then AdamW's learning rate and weight decay can be reused, for matrix and non-matrix parameters alike.

Lab L1 implements both and checks the RMS of the scaled update.

Training impact

  • Fewer FLOPs for the same loss. About 2× in Moonlight's compute-optimal comparison; Jordan's 1.5B run needed 25% less time.
  • A matrix-aware update. The optimizer now depends on parameter shape; which tensors count as "hidden matrices" is part of the recipe (lab L1 splits them in hidden_matrices()).
  • Optimizer mismatch. AdamW-pre-trained models fine-tuned with Muon, and the reverse, did worse than matching optimizers; with a mismatched pre-training optimizer, Muon fine-tuning showed no clear gain. Post-training (Parts V to IX) inherits the choice.
  • Systems work. Sharded optimizers (deck 09) assumed element-wise updates; Muon needs whole matrices.

Afterlife

  • Kimi K2 trained a 1T-parameter MoE with Muon. Attention logits blew up past 1,000 with plain Muon, so it adds QK-Clip, which rescales a head's query and key weights when its largest logit passes a threshold: architecture deck 33.
