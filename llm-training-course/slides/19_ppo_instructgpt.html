PPO and InstructGPT

Proximal Policy Optimization (2017) became the optimizer of RLHF because it takes several cheap gradient steps per batch without letting the policy run away. InstructGPT (2022) combined SFT, a reward model and PPO into the pipeline behind ChatGPT, and showed that a 1.3B model trained this way was preferred to 175B GPT-3.

Problem: policy gradients are noisy and fragile

  • The policy gradient raises the log-probability of actions in proportion to their advantage A: how much better the action was than expected.
  • Estimates of A from sampled returns have high variance; a learned value function V lowers it but adds bias.
  • Each batch of samples is expensive (for a language model, generating text), so one would like several steps on it. But the samples come from the old policy, and too large a step can collapse the policy, after which it samples only bad data.
  • TRPO bounded each step with a KL constraint solved by second-order methods. PPO gets a similar effect with first-order steps.

Generalized advantage estimation

δt = rt + γV(st+1) − V(st)    Ât = Σl≥0 (γλ)l δt+l
  • A weighted sum of one-step TD errors. λ = 0 uses one step and trusts V: low variance, biased when V is wrong. λ = 1 uses the whole sampled return: unbiased, high variance.
  • An intermediate λ in [0.9, 0.99] usually worked best in the GAE paper's control tasks.
  • In RLHF the reward model scores the whole answer once, at the last token; the per-token KL penalty adds a small reward at every token. InstructGPT initialized its value model from the reward model.

PPO: clip the probability ratio

LCLIP = E[min(ρtÂt, clip(ρt, 1−ε, 1+ε)Ât)]

ρt = πθ(at|st) / πold(at|st) is the probability ratio between the new and the old policy. The min makes the objective a pessimistic bound: once the ratio has moved by ε in the direction the advantage favours, the gradient is zero. ε = 0.2. Several epochs of minibatch steps on each batch; the paper's KL-penalty variant did worse than clipping.

PPO for language models: four models in memory

R(x, y) = rφ(x, y) − β log πθ(y | x) / πSFT(y | x)
  • Policy (trained), value model (trained), reference SFT model (frozen, for the KL term), reward model (frozen). Each is the size of a language model, which is why RLHF is costly and why later methods drop some of them (DPO, deck 22; GRPO, deck 26).
  • An episode is a generated answer; an action is a token. The KL term keeps the policy where the reward model is reliable and keeps its text fluent.
  • Lab L5 runs PPO with this reward on a toy policy and checks that clipping stops large steps.

InstructGPT: three steps from GPT-3

  • Prompts came from the OpenAI API and from labelers; about 40 contractors wrote demonstrations and ranked outputs.
  • Reward model: 6B, because 175B was unstable. A labeler ranks K = 4 to 9 outputs, giving K(K−1)/2 comparisons; all of them go into one batch element so the model does not overfit to correlated pairs.
  • PPO-ptx adds the pre-training log-likelihood to the RL objective, γ Ex∼Dpretrain[log π(x)], to win back abilities RLHF loses.

Evidence: InstructGPT

  • Size is not everything: outputs of the 1.3B InstructGPT model were preferred to those of 175B GPT-3, with over 100 times fewer parameters.
  • 175B InstructGPT outputs were preferred to GPT-3's 85 ± 3% of the time, and to few-shot prompted GPT-3's 71 ± 4%.
  • Truthfulness: on closed-domain tasks, InstructGPT made up information 21% of the time against 41% for GPT-3.
  • Toxicity: about 25% fewer toxic outputs than GPT-3 when prompted to be respectful; no improvement on bias benchmarks.
Ouyang et al. 2022 (InstructGPT), arXiv:2203.02155 §4.1–4.2, Figs. 1, 3 · bibliography

The alignment tax

  • PPO alone lowered scores on public NLP benchmarks such as SQuAD, DROP, HellaSwag and WMT French-to-English translation: the alignment tax.
  • Mixing pre-training gradients in (PPO-ptx) removed most of the drop without losing labeler preference, but the model still lagged GPT-3 on DROP, SQuADv2 and translation.
  • The labelers' preferences, not a notion of human values, define the target: the paper says so and discusses whose preferences those are.
Ouyang et al. 2022 (InstructGPT), arXiv:2203.02155 §4.2, §5.2–5.3 · bibliography

Training impact and afterlife

  • The three-step pipeline (SFT, reward model, PPO) became the template for chat models: ChatGPT, Anthropic's assistants and Llama 2-Chat (deck 20).
  • PPO is heavy: four models, many hyperparameters, generation inside the training loop. Simpler optimizers followed: DPO removes the reward model and the RL loop (deck 22); GRPO removes the value model (deck 26).
  • Clipping survived into GRPO and its successors, which keep the PPO ratio and its clip.
