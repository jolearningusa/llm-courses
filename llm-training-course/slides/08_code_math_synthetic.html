Problem: the abilities you want are rare on the web

  • Code, mathematics and step-by-step explanations are a small share of web text, and much of what exists is noisy: snippets without context, auto-generated pages, half-finished answers.
  • Scaling general web data improves them slowly. Three answers: filter harder for the right content, mine the web with a domain classifier, or generate the data with a stronger model.
  • And when to show it: throughout pre-training, or at the end, when the learning rate decays (deck 01)?

Evidence and caveats: phi-1

  • HumanEval pass@1 50.6% and MBPP 55.5% at 1.3B; phi-1-small (350M) reached 45% on HumanEval.
  • Contamination check: the authors pruned CodeExercises of problems similar to HumanEval (42.5K to 354K of 879.5K removed) and retrained; HumanEval stayed between 45.1% and 50.6%.
  • A separate team wrote 50 unconventional problems the model could not have seen.
  • Stated limits: Python only, less general knowledge, weaker on long or flawed prompts; some generation details withheld.

Pass@k and contamination are taught in deck 32; architecture deck 33 follows the Phi line to Phi-3.

Mid-training: the best data at the end

  • Llama 3: over the last 40M tokens, anneal the learning rate linearly to 0, upsample very high-quality sources, and average checkpoints. Annealing on high-quality code and math raised the 8B model's GSM8K by 24.0% and MATH by 6.4%; for 405B the gain was negligible.
  • Judging a dataset by annealing: take an 8B model halfway through training, anneal it to 0 over 40B tokens with the new data at 30% weight, and compare. Cheaper than a scaling-law study per dataset.
  • OLMo 2: a second stage of 5–10% of training FLOPs on Dolmino Mix 1124 (filtered web, curated sources and synthetic math), the learning rate decayed to zero; several annealing runs with different data orders are averaged ("souping").

Training impact and afterlife

  • Data quality buys size. phi-1 at 1.3B and a few billion tokens rivaled much larger code models; Phi-3 extended the recipe (architecture deck 33).
  • Classifier-driven recall. The DeepSeekMath loop (seed, classifier, recall, expand) is the general recipe for any domain the web under-represents.
  • Stages, not one mixture. Pre-training now ends with a decay on curated and synthetic data, matching WSD (deck 01): the stable phase learns from the broad web, the decay from the best data.
  • Synthetic data needs checks. Contamination and the generator's errors (phi-1 notes GPT-3.5's high error rate) travel into the model.
