Domain-adaptive and continual pre-training

A trained model meets new data: a domain it saw little of, or a fresh crawl after the run. Gururangan et al. showed that a second phase of pre-training on the domain pays off; Ibrahim et al. showed how to keep training a large model on new data without forgetting the old, for a fraction of retraining.

Problem: new data, an old model

  • A general model under-serves domains that were a small share of its data: biomedical papers, legal text, a new language, math.
  • Data keeps arriving: retraining from scratch on old plus new data repeats the whole cost every time.
  • Continuing to train risks forgetting: the old distribution's loss rises as the model fits the new one.
  • The schedule is the trap: a run ends with the learning rate decayed (deck 01). Raising it again for new data destabilizes what was learned.

Don't Stop Pretraining: DAPT and TAPT

  • Start from RoBERTa-base. Four domains (biomedical, computer science, news, reviews), two classification tasks each.
  • DAPT: continue masked-LM training on a large domain corpus, 12.5K steps, about one pass.
  • TAPT: continue on the task's own unlabeled training text, 100 epochs. Much smaller, closer to the task.
  • Control: continue on an unrelated domain (¬DAPT), to separate "more training" from "the right data."

Continual pre-training of LLMs: three ingredients

Re-warm the learning rate for the new data, re-decay it, and replay a share of the old data. The infinite schedule (warm up once, hold a constant rate, anneal only when a model is needed) avoids re-warming; it is close to WSD (deck 01).

Training impact and afterlife

  • Continued pre-training is now a stage, not an exception. Mid-training on curated data (deck 08) and long-context extension (deck 11) are both continual pre-training with a new mixture.
  • Domain models start from general ones. DeepSeekMath continued DeepSeek-Coder-Base-v1.5 for 500B tokens with 56% math, keeping code, arXiv and web text in the mix: domain data plus replay.
  • Schedules that can continue. WSD and infinite schedules keep a checkpoint at a constant rate, so new data does not need a re-warm.
  • Measure both sides: the new domain and the old benchmarks, as Llama 3 does for short-context scores during its long-context stages.
