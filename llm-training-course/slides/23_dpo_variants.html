DPO variants: IPO, KTO, ORPO and SimPO

Within a year of DPO, four variants each changed one assumption. IPO (2023) replaced Bradley-Terry with a squared loss that cannot overfit certain preferences. KTO (2024) learns from single answers marked good or bad. ORPO (2024) folds preference tuning into SFT without a reference model. SimPO (2024) uses the average log-likelihood as the reward, with a margin.

IPO: a target gap instead of an unbounded one

LIPO = E[ (h − 1/(2τ))2 ]
h = log π(yw)/πref(yw) − log π(yl)/πref(yl)
  • ΨPO: maximize E[Ψ(p(y ≻ y′))] − τ KL. Ψ = log(q/(1−q)) gives RLHF and DPO; Ψ = identity gives IPO, which needs no Bradley-Terry model.
  • Why: if y always beats y′, Bradley-Terry needs an infinite reward gap, so DPO drives π(y′) to 0 for any τ: the KL term stops working. With finite data, one observed win can be enough.
  • IPO regresses the log-ratio gap to 1/(2τ). For two answers with a certain preference and a uniform reference, π(yw) = σ(1/(2τ)), back to the reference as τ grows.
  • Evidence: only bandit examples with 3 actions; the paper leaves language models to later work. Lab L6 reproduces the two-answer case.

KTO: good or bad, no pairs

desirable: v = λD σ(β(rθ − z0))
undesirable: v = λU σ(β(z0 − rθ))
  • rθ = log πθ/πref. Inspired by prospect theory: value is judged relative to a reference point z0, an estimate of KL(πθ ‖ πref) taken from mismatched pairs in the batch and not backpropagated. The loss is λy − v.
  • Evidence: matched or beat DPO from 1B to 30B (Pythia, Llama); significantly better at Llama 7B and 30B. Still beat DPO after discarding up to 90% of the desirable examples at 7B.
  • At 13B and 30B, KTO without SFT was competitive with SFT then KTO; DPO without SFT rambled.
Ethayarajh et al. 2024 (KTO), arXiv:2402.01306 §3–4, Eq. 7, Figs. 3–5 · bibliography

ORPO: preference tuning inside SFT

LORPO = LSFT − λ log σ( log odds(yw) / odds(yl) )
odds(y|x) = P(y|x) / (1 − P(y|x))
  • One stage from the base model: the SFT loss on the chosen answer plus an odds-ratio penalty against the rejected one. No reference model, so about half the forward passes of DPO per batch.
  • λ was 0.1 to 0.25 depending on the model.
  • Evidence: Mistral-ORPO-β 7B, trained on UltraFeedback alone, reached 12.20% on AlpacaEval 2.0 and 7.32 on MT-Bench; Llama-2 7B with ORPO 9.44% on AlpacaEval 2.0.
Hong et al. 2024 (ORPO), arXiv:2403.07691 §4, §6, Eqs. 4–7, Table 1 · bibliography

SimPO: average log-likelihood with a margin

LSimPO = −log σ(β log π(yw)/|yw| − β log π(yl)/|yl| − γ)
  • Mismatch: generation favours high average likelihood, but DPO's reward is a summed log-ratio. In about half of the training pairs that DPO's reward ordered correctly, the chosen answer still had lower average likelihood.
  • Dividing by length removes the bias toward long answers; without it AlpacaEval 2 LC fell from 21.5 to 11.9 on Mistral-Base. The margin γ asks the chosen answer to win clearly.
  • No reference model: about 20% less run time and 10% less peak memory than DPO. β of 2.0–2.5, γ of 0.5–1.5.
Meng et al. 2024 (SimPO), arXiv:2405.14734 §2, §4.4, Eqs. 4–6, Tables 5, 8, Fig. 4–5 · bibliography

Evidence: SimPO, and comparing variants

  • SimPO beat DPO by up to 6.4 points on AlpacaEval 2 LC and up to 7.5 on Arena-Hard, and the best other baseline by 3.6–4.8 on AlpacaEval 2 LC.
  • Gemma-2-9B-it with SimPO reached 72.4% AlpacaEval 2 LC and 59.1% on Arena-Hard, with training data ranked by a stronger reward model (ArmoRM).
  • Read with care: each paper tunes its own method on its own setup, and chat benchmarks judged by GPT-4 favour some styles (deck 32). Compare variants under one shared setup and budget before choosing.
Meng et al. 2024 (SimPO), arXiv:2405.14734 §1, §4.1, Tables 1, 4 · bibliography
