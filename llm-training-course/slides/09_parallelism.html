Parallelism for model builders

A model you want to train rarely fits on one GPU, and one GPU would take centuries anyway. ZeRO shards the training state, Megatron-LM splits each layer, GPipe splits the stack, and Megatron's 3D recipe combines all three. This deck is the training view: what each choice costs a model builder.

Problem: the state, the layer and the clock

  • State. Mixed-precision Adam keeps 2 + 2 + 12 = 16 bytes per parameter: fp16 weights and gradients, fp32 master weights, momentum and variance. A 70B model needs 1.12 TB before a single activation.
  • Layer. Activations grow with sequence length, batch and width, and one layer's matrices can outgrow a GPU.
  • Clock. 6ND for Llama 3 405B is 3.8 × 1025 FLOPs: thousands of GPUs must work on one step (lab L0).
  • Each split trades memory for communication. The kernels and collectives behind them are taught in compute decks 25, 26 and 27.

Megatron-LM: split each layer across GPUs

  • Split the MLP's first matrix by columns so each GPU applies GeLU to its own slice, the second by rows; attention splits by heads. One all-reduce in the forward pass and one in the backward pass per block: four per layer.
  • 8.3B parameters on 512 V100s with 8-way tensor parallelism, 15.1 PFLOP/s sustained, 76% scaling efficiency against a single GPU at 39 TFLOP/s.
  • The 8.3B model set WikiText-103 perplexity 10.81 (from 15.79) and LAMBADA 66.51%. For BERT-style models past BERT-Large, the order of layer norm and residual mattered (architecture deck 17).
Shoeybi et al. 2019 (Megatron-LM), arXiv:1909.08053 §3, §5 · bibliography

GPipe: split the stack, fill it with micro-batches

K stages hold consecutive layers; a batch is cut into M micro-batches that flow through them. The bubble is (K − 1)/(M + K − 1) of the step, negligible at M ≥ 4K. Recomputing activations cuts peak memory from O(N × L) to O(N + L/K × N/M). Evidence: a 6B, 128-layer translation Transformer over 100+ languages.

Huang et al. 2018 (GPipe), arXiv:1811.06965 §2.3 · bibliography

3D parallelism: tensor inside a node, pipeline across

  • Rule: use tensor parallelism up to the GPUs in one server (8 on DGX A100), pipeline across servers, data parallelism outside both. Tensor parallelism needs NVLink bandwidth; pipeline sends only activations at stage boundaries.
  • Interleaving: give each device v smaller chunks of layers; the bubble falls from (p − 1)/m to (p − 1)/(m·v) of the compute time, for v times more pipeline messages.
  • Evidence: a 1T-parameter model on 3,072 A100s at 502 PFLOP/s, 163 TFLOP/s per GPU, 52% of peak.
  • Planning time: with recomputation a step costs about 8TP FLOPs, so days ≈ 8TP / (nX): 34 days for GPT-3 175B on 1,024 GPUs, 84 days for the 1T model on 3,072. Lab L0 reproduces both.
Narayanan et al. 2021, arXiv:2104.04473 §2.2, §3, §5.1, Table 1 · bibliography

Activation memory: sequence parallel and selective recompute

sbh · (34 + 5as/h) bytes per layer
  • 16-bit activations for sequence s, micro-batch b, hidden h, heads a. Tensor parallelism alone gives sbh(10 + 24/t + 5as/(ht)): layer norms and dropout stay replicated.
  • Sequence parallelism splits those parts along the sequence, so all of it divides by t.
  • Selective recomputation stores everything except the attention scores and softmax (the 5as/h term), which are large but cheap to redo: 34sbh/t.
  • Together: 5× less activation memory, over 90% less recomputation overhead, 54.2% MFU for a 530B model against 42.1% with full recomputation.
Korthikanti et al. 2022, arXiv:2205.05198 eqs. 1–4, Table 2, Abstract · bibliography
