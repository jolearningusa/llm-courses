Distilling LLMs: MiniLLM, on-policy distillation, Gemma 2, R1's distilled models

Deck 29's methods train a student on fixed sequences with a forward KL. For generative language models two things change: the student must generate from its own prefixes, and a small student cannot cover everything the teacher can say. MiniLLM and GKD (2023) train on the student's own samples and choose the divergence. Gemma 2 (2024) distilled during pre-training, and DeepSeek-R1 (2025) distilled reasoning by plain SFT.

Problem: fixed data and the forward KL

  • Train-inference mismatch: supervised KD scores the student on gold or teacher prefixes, but at inference the student continues its own, possibly wrong, prefixes (exposure bias).
  • Too little capacity: the forward KL, KL(p‖q) = Ep[log p − log q], is infinite wherever the teacher has mass and the student has none, so a small student spreads out to cover every mode, including the regions between them.
  • In open-ended generation those in-between regions are text the teacher would rarely write.

Forward against reverse KL

Fits from lab L8. Forward KL (supervised KD) puts the student between the modes with a wide spread. Reverse KL, KL(q‖p) = Eq[log q − log p], punishes the student for mass where the teacher has little, so it settles on one mode: here the lighter one, nearer its start.

MiniLLM: reverse KL by policy gradient

minθ KL(qθ ‖ p) over whole responses
  • The gradient is a policy gradient: sample from the student, use −log(q/p) per token as the reward, as in RLHF without a reward model.
  • Single-step decomposition: compute the next token's term exactly over the vocabulary, which lowers variance.
  • Teacher-mixed sampling: sample from a mix of teacher and student (α = 0.2) to stop the student exploiting the reward with degenerate text.
  • Length normalization: remove the bias toward short responses.

Evidence: MiniLLM

  • Setup: students GPT-2 120M to 760M, OPT 1.3B to 6.7B and LLaMA-7B; teachers GPT-2 1.5B, OPT-13B and LLaMA-13B; instruction-following data.
  • MiniLLM beat SFT without a teacher, word-level KD and sequence-level KD on Rouge-L and on GPT-4 scores, across the three families.
  • Its students had lower exposure bias, were better calibrated and did better on long responses.
  • The gains held from 120M to 13B parameters.

GKD: choose the data and the divergence

(1 − λ) E(x,y)~data[D(p ‖ q)(y|x)]
+ λ Ex, y~q[D(p ‖ q)(y|x)]
  • λ is the fraction of student-generated outputs: λ = 0 is supervised KD, λ = 1 is on-policy GKD. Gradients do not flow through the sampling.
  • D is a per-token divergence: forward KL, reverse KL, or the generalized JSD(β), which moves between the two as β goes from 0 to 1.
  • The teacher scores the student's samples token by token, so the student learns where its own outputs go wrong. The same loss can be added to an RL objective.
Agarwal et al. 2023 (GKD), arXiv:2306.13649 §3 · Lab L8 (JSD limits) · bibliography

Evidence: GKD

  • Setup: T5-XL teacher; T5-small, base and large students; summarization (XSum), translation (WMT en→de), arithmetic (GSM8K), and instruction tuning on FLAN.
  • On-policy GKD gave relative gains of 2.1× on summarization, 1.7× on translation and 1.9× on arithmetic over the gains of baseline KD methods.
  • On XSum, on-policy GKD with 5% of the data and no ground-truth summaries beat supervised KD trained on the full data set.
  • The best divergence depended on the task: mode-seeking ones with temperature sampling on XSum, JSD(0.1) on WMT, forward KL on GSM8K, reverse KL on FLAN instruction tuning.
Agarwal et al. 2023 (GKD), arXiv:2306.13649 §4, Figs. 1–2 · bibliography

Gemma 2: distillation in pre-training

  • The 2B and 9B models were pre-trained on a larger teacher's next-token distribution instead of one-hot next tokens; the 27B was trained from scratch. Tokens: 2T, 8T and 13T.
  • The small models were trained on over 50× the compute-optimal number of tokens; a full distribution per token gives far more signal than one sampled next token.
  • Post-training also used on-policy distillation in SFT, citing Agarwal et al. and Gu et al.

DeepSeek-R1's distilled models

  • DeepSeek fine-tuned open dense models (Qwen2.5-Math-7B, Qwen2.5-32B and Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct) on reasoning traces generated with R1: sequence-level distillation (deck 29) by plain SFT, with no RL stage.
  • Learning rates in the paper: 8e-5 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B, 6e-5 for Qwen2.5-32B, 2e-5 for Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct.
  • The paper reports that the distilled models inherit strong reasoning; their scores are in its supplementary material.
  • Only the teacher's text is needed, so the student may use a different tokenizer, unlike logit distillation.

Training impact and afterlife

  • Three ways to distill: teacher text (cheap, any tokenizer; R1), teacher logits on fixed text (Gemma 2 pre-training), teacher logits on student samples (MiniLLM, GKD; a teacher forward pass at every step).
  • The divergence is a choice: forward KL covers, reverse KL seeks a mode; which is better depends on the task and on how the student will be sampled.
  • On-policy distillation is RL with a dense reward: the same sampling loop as decks 19 and 26, with the teacher's per-token log-ratio in place of a reward model.
  • Small open models are often distilled, so evaluation must ask what was learned and what was copied: deck 32.
