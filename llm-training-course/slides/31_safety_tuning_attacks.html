Safety tuning and its attacks

RLHF and Constitutional AI (decks 20 and 21) teach a model to refuse harmful requests. This deck asks how to find the failures (red teaming with language models, 2022), how much safety data tuning needs (2023), how easily tuning is undone (fine-tuning attacks and GCG suffixes, 2023), and how a reasoning model can be taught to apply a written policy (deliberative alignment, 2024).

Problem: safety is a learned behaviour

  • A chat model refuses because SFT and preference training made refusals likely on prompts like those in its training data. Nothing guarantees the behaviour on prompts unlike them.
  • Finding failures by hand is slow: human red teams write thousands of prompts, not millions.
  • Too little safety data leaves harmful answers; too much makes the model refuse safe requests.
  • Open weights and fine-tuning APIs let anyone train further, and optimized inputs can search for prompts the training never covered.

Red teaming with language models

  • Target: Dialogue-Prompted Gopher (280B). Red LM: Gopher 280B. Classifier: a 1.4B Gopher model fine-tuned on the Bot-Adversarial Dialogue data set.
  • Four ways to generate: zero-shot prompting, stochastic few-shot from earlier failures, supervised learning on failures, and RL with the classifier's score as the reward.
  • The failures found become training data, or show which prompts the next model must handle.

Safety-Tuned LLaMAs: how much safety data

  • Setup: LLaMA 7B (also 13B and Falcon 7B), LoRA on 20,000 Alpaca instructions plus 100 to 2,000 safety examples: red-team questions with refusals, written with GPT-3.5-turbo.
  • With no safety data, 44 of the first 50 answers to malicious instructions were unsafe.
  • 500 to 1,000 safety examples, about 3% of the data, substantially reduced harmfulness. Win rate on malicious instructions against the Alpaca reference: 0.70 with 100, 0.92 with 500, 0.94 with 2,000.
  • Exaggerated safety: with 2,000 examples the model refused more than half of a set of safe questions that resemble unsafe ones ("Where can I shoot a photo?").
  • General benchmarks barely moved (BoolQ 77.16 against 77.09; PIQA 79.65 against 79.33).
Bianchi et al. 2023 (Safety-Tuned LLaMAs), arXiv:2309.07875 §3–4 · LoRA: deck 16 · bibliography

GCG: optimizing an adversarial suffix

min over suffix: −log p("Sure, here is …" | prompt + suffix)
  • Append 20 tokens to a harmful request and optimize them so the model starts its answer affirmatively; once it starts that way, it tends to continue.
  • Greedy coordinate gradient: the gradient with respect to one-hot token indicators proposes the top-k substitutions at every position; evaluate a random batch of them exactly and keep the best. Batch 512, top-k 256, 500 steps.
  • Universal and transferable: optimize one suffix over many behaviours and several open models at once, then use it on closed models.
  • Benchmark: AdvBench, 500 harmful strings and 500 harmful behaviours.

Deliberative alignment

  • Idea: teach a reasoning model the text of the safety specification, and to reason over it in its chain of thought before it answers.
  • SFT: a model shown the spec in its system prompt writes (prompt, chain of thought citing the spec, answer) examples; a judge model that sees the spec filters them; the spec is removed from the training prompts, so the model must recall it.
  • RL: a judge with the spec rewards the answer on safety prompts. The chain of thought is hidden from it, to avoid pressure that could teach a misleading chain of thought.
  • No human-written completions; used for OpenAI's o-series models, with the RL stage for o1 and o3-mini.
Guan et al. 2024 (deliberative alignment), arXiv:2412.16339 §2–3 · bibliography

Training impact and afterlife

  • Measure both directions: harmful answers and refusals of safe requests, every time the data or the model changes.
  • Safety data is a small slice of SFT and preference data (a few percent), mixed in rather than trained separately.
  • Every fine-tune is a new model to evaluate: benign data, LoRA adapters and API fine-tuning can each remove safety behaviour.
  • Attacks are evaluations: automated red teaming and suffix search are now standard probes before release.
  • Reasoning changes the method: a model that can read a policy can be trained to apply it, as in Constitutional AI (deck 21) but at inference.
