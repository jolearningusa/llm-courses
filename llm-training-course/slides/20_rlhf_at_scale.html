RLHF at scale: Anthropic HH, Llama 2-Chat and overoptimization

In 2022 and 2023 RLHF moved from summaries to general assistants. Anthropic trained a helpful and harmless assistant with weekly rounds of new human data; Meta trained Llama 2-Chat on over a million comparisons with two reward models. Gao et al. measured what happens when a policy optimizes a reward model too hard.

Problem: an assistant, not one task

  • A general assistant must be helpful on any request and refuse harmful ones. The two pull against each other: the most harmless answer is often a refusal.
  • The policy changes as it trains, so comparisons collected from an early model describe text the later model no longer writes.
  • A reward model is a proxy. The harder the policy optimizes it, the more it finds the proxy's errors.

Anthropic HH: helpful, harmless and online

  • Data: crowdworkers chatted with models and picked the better of two replies at each turn: 44K helpfulness comparisons and 42K red-teaming (harmlessness) comparisons in the static set.
  • Online: 22K more helpfulness comparisons collected roughly weekly over five weeks, each round from the newest policies. They filled in the upper tail of quality, where the earlier data had few examples.
  • Preference model pre-training: before fine-tuning on the human comparisons, the preference model was trained on comparisons from public data (following Askell et al. 2021), which made it more sample-efficient.
  • Models from 13M to 52B; PPO with a small KL coefficient (0.001).

Evidence: Anthropic HH

  • Reward and KL: during RL, the preference-model score rose roughly linearly in √KL from the initial policy.
  • Alignment bonus at scale: RLHF lowered zero-shot NLP scores for small models but raised them for 13B and 52B models, the reverse of InstructGPT's tax.
  • Tension: early policies gave exaggerated, evasive answers to sensitive questions, which the authors put down to over-optimizing harmlessness and under-optimizing helpfulness.
  • The 52B preference model reached 86% accuracy on the HHH evaluations.

Llama 2-Chat: two reward models

L = − log σ(r(x, yc) − r(x, yr) − m(rating))
  • Over 1.4 million new Meta comparisons, 2.9 million with open data. Annotators rated how much better the chosen reply was on four levels; the margin m grows with that level, so clear preferences separate more.
  • Separate Helpfulness and Safety reward models, because the two goals sometimes trade off and one model struggled to do well on both. Both start from the chat model's own checkpoint, so the reward model knows what the policy knows.
  • Architecture of the base models: architecture deck 28.

5 / 10

Llama 2-Chat: rejection sampling, then PPO

  • Five rounds (RLHF-V1 to V5), each with new comparisons from the newest model.
  • Rejection sampling fine-tuning: sample K answers per prompt, keep the one the reward model likes best, fine-tune on it. Up to V4 only this; afterwards PPO on top of the rejection-sampled checkpoint.
  • Combining the reward models: use the safety score when the prompt is tagged unsafe or the safety score is below 0.15 (precision 0.89, recall 0.55 on a safety test set), else the helpfulness score. Scores are whitened before PPO.
  • KL coefficient β = 0.01 for 7B and 13B, 0.005 for 34B and 70B.

Overoptimization: the proxy keeps rising, the gold reward does not

A 6B "gold" reward model stands in for people; proxy reward models from 3M to 3B are trained on its labels, and policies optimize the proxies. With d = √KL: best-of-n gold reward d(α − βd); RL gold reward d(α − β log d). The coefficients change smoothly, roughly with the log of the reward model's size.

Evidence: Gao et al.

  • Bigger reward models overoptimize later and less: the gold-reward peak moves to higher KL and higher reward as proxy size grows.
  • Data: with fewer than about 2,000 comparisons the proxies were near chance; more data helped in a smooth trend.
  • The KL penalty behaved like early stopping: it changed where training stopped on the gold-reward against KL curve, not the curve itself.
  • Limits: synthetic labels from a reward model, not people; only two policy sizes.
  • Lab L5 reproduces the shape: a linear reward model, right where the data is, sends the policy where the gold reward falls.

Training impact and afterlife

  • Iterated online RLHF with fresh comparisons from each model became standard; a fixed preference set goes stale as the policy moves.
  • Rejection sampling (best-of-n then SFT) is simple and stable and stayed in recipes next to or instead of PPO; deck 25 meets the same idea as a step of reasoning training (STaR).
  • Reward models are tracked like any model: held-out accuracy, calibration, and gold-reward checks against the KL spent. Reward hacking returns in RL for reasoning, where a verifier replaces the reward model (Part IX).
  • AI labels instead of human ones: deck 21.
