GRPO and REINFORCE baselines: DeepSeekMath and RLOO

PPO needs a value model as large as the policy. In 2024 two papers dropped it. DeepSeekMath introduced Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO): sample several answers per question and use their mean reward as the baseline. RLOO revisited REINFORCE and found that, for language models, the simpler estimator beats PPO.

Problem: the value model is expensive and hard to train

  • PPO for language models keeps a value model the size of the policy, trained alongside it (deck 19).
  • The reward usually arrives once, at the last token, so a per-token value function is hard to learn: most tokens get credit only through it.
  • The value model is there to reduce variance. A cheaper baseline is the reward of other answers to the same prompt.

GRPO: the group is the baseline

Âi,t = (ri − mean(r)) / std(r)
  • Every token t of answer i gets the same advantage. The PPO clipped ratio per token, averaged over each answer's tokens (1/|oi|), then over the G answers.
  • The KL to the reference is added to the loss, not to the reward, with the estimator πref/π − log(πref/π) − 1, which is always positive.
  • A process-supervised variant gives each token the sum of normalized step rewards from that step on.

One frame for SFT, RFT, DPO, PPO and GRPO

  • DeepSeekMath writes every method as three choices: the data (fixed, or sampled from the current policy), the reward (a rule or a model), and the gradient coefficient that scales ∇ log π for each token.
  • SFT: coefficient 1 on human data. Rejection-sampling fine-tuning: 1 on correct samples, 0 otherwise. Online RFT: the same on the current policy's samples. GRPO: the normalized advantage, which also pushes wrong answers down.
  • Online beat offline, and GRPO beat online RFT.
  • RL raised Maj@K but not Pass@K: it made correct answers the model could already produce more likely, rather than adding new ones.
Shao et al. 2024 (DeepSeekMath, GRPO), arXiv:2402.03300 §5.2, Eq. 5, Table 10, Figs. 5, 7 · bibliography

RLOO: back to REINFORCE

∇J ≈ (1/k) Σi [ ri − (1/(k−1)) Σj≠i rj ] ∇ log π(yi | x)
  • Treat the whole answer as one action (the reward comes once, transitions are deterministic): a bandit. REINFORCE with, for each of k samples, the mean reward of the other k − 1 as its baseline. Unbiased, no learned critic.
  • An SFT model is already concentrated on few tokens, so the variance PPO guards against is small. GAE with λ < 1 added bias and did worse than λ = 1; clipping was active in under 5% of updates, and removing it did not hurt.

Training impact and afterlife

  • GRPO became the default for reasoning RL: DeepSeek-R1 (deck 27) and most open replications use it, with a rule-based reward instead of a reward model.
  • The value model went away; PPO's clip stayed. Memory goes to more samples per prompt instead.
  • Its normalizations were questioned: dividing by the group's std and by each answer's length biases training (Dr. GRPO, deck 28). Without them, GRPO's advantage is RLOO's up to a constant factor.
  • Lab L7 checks that group advantages sum to zero and that a group with all-equal rewards gives no gradient.
