Low-precision training

Fewer bits per number means less memory, less traffic and faster matrix units, if training still converges. Mixed precision made FP16 safe in 2017; FP8 formats followed in 2022; DeepSeek-V3 trained a 671B model with FP8 mixed precision.

Problem: small numbers vanish, large ones overflow

  • FP16 has 5 exponent bits: any magnitude below 2−24 becomes zero, and above 65,504 it overflows.
  • In FP32 training of their networks, about 5% of weight gradients had exponents below −24, and 67% of SSD's activation gradients were zero in FP16.
  • Adding an update to a weight 2,048 times larger shifts it to zero in FP16, so a model can stop learning without any error.
  • Bit layouts, rounding and how matrix units accumulate: compute deck 13. This deck covers the training recipes built on them.

Mixed precision: three techniques

  • FP32 master weights receive every update; FP16 copies do the math.
  • Loss scaling by a constant S shifts gradients into FP16's range (S = 8 for SSD, which diverged without it); the paper tested 8 to 32K and named automatic scaling as future work.
  • FP16 products, FP32 sums in matrix multiplies and reductions.

Evidence and the switch to BF16

  • Image classification, detection, speech, translation, language modeling and GANs matched their FP32 baselines; overall memory roughly halved even though the master copy adds 50% to weight memory.
  • The 16 bytes per parameter of decks 09 and L0 come from this recipe: 2 + 2 for FP16 weights and gradients, 12 for FP32 master weights and Adam's two moments.
  • BF16 keeps FP32's 8 exponent bits with 7 mantissa bits, so gradients rarely underflow and loss scaling is usually dropped. Llama 3 405B reports its utilization as BF16 MFU, and accumulates gradients across micro-batches in FP32.

DeepSeek-V3: fine-grained FP8 at 671B

  • Fine-grained scales: one scale per 1 × 128 tile of activations (per token, per 128 channels) and per 128 × 128 block of weights, computed online from the current maximum, not from past steps. Outliers then affect only their own tile.
  • E4M3 everywhere, including gradients, since small tiles make the extra range of E5M2 unnecessary.
  • Accumulation: H800 tensor cores keep about 14 bits when summing FP8 products (up to nearly 2% relative error at K = 4096), so partial sums move to FP32 every 128 elements.
  • Kept in BF16 or FP32: embedding, output head, MoE gating, normalization and attention. Master weights and gradients FP32; Adam moments BF16; activations cached in FP8.
DeepSeek-V3, arXiv:2412.19437 §3.3 · bibliography

Evidence and afterlife

  • On two smaller models (similar to DeepSeek-V2-Lite and V2) trained for about 1T tokens, FP8 loss stayed within 0.25% of BF16.
  • The full run: 14.8T tokens for 2.788M H800 GPU hours (architecture deck 31 covers the model).
  • The trend: each step cut bits and moved the safety net. FP32 master weights (2017), per-tensor scales (2022), per-tile scales with FP32 promotion (2024). What stays high-precision is the sensitive part: sums, optimizer state, normalization and the first and last layers.
  • Number formats, rounding and accumulation on current hardware: compute deck 13.
