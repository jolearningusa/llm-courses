Instruction tuning: FLAN, T0 and the Flan Collection

A base model continues text; it does not follow requests. FLAN and T0 (2021) rewrote dozens of labeled NLP datasets as natural-language instructions and fine-tuned on them, and the models answered unseen kinds of task zero-shot. Flan-T5 and the Flan Collection (2022) scaled the idea to 1,836 tasks.

Problem: a base model needs a prompt engineer

  • GPT-3 did well few-shot but much worse zero-shot on tasks whose prompt does not look like pre-training text, such as reading comprehension or NLI.
  • Labeled NLP datasets already existed by the hundreds, each in its own input format.
  • Idea: describe each task in words, fine-tune on many of them, and test on kinds of task the model never saw. If it works, the model has learned to follow instructions, not just the tasks.
Wei et al. 2021 (FLAN), arXiv:2109.01652 §1 · bibliography

FLAN: 62 datasets, 12 clusters, held-out evaluation

  • LaMDA-PT, a 137B decoder-only model, fine-tuned on 62 datasets in 12 task clusters, each with 10 templates (up to 3 of which turn the task around, such as writing a review for a given sentiment).
  • To test a cluster, train on all other clusters: one checkpoint per evaluated cluster, so no task of that type is seen.
Wei et al. 2021 (FLAN), arXiv:2109.01652 §2, Fig. 3 · bibliography

Evidence: FLAN and T0

  • FLAN 137B zero-shot beat zero-shot GPT-3 175B on 20 of 25 datasets, and beat few-shot GPT-3 by a large margin on ANLI, RTE, BoolQ, AI2-ARC, OpenbookQA and StoryCloze.
  • More clusters help: average held-out score rose from 49.9 with one training cluster to 63.5 with seven.
  • Scale decides: instruction tuning hurt held-out tasks for models of 8B and smaller, and helped at 68B and 137B.
  • T0 (T5+LM 11B, prompts from the P3 pool: 2,073 prompts for 177 datasets) matched or beat GPT-3 on 9 of 11 held-out datasets at about 16× fewer parameters. More prompts per dataset raised the median on 8 of 11 and narrowed the spread across prompts on 7 of 11.
Wei et al. 2021 (FLAN), arXiv:2109.01652 §3, §4.1–4.2, Figs. 6–7 · Sanh et al. 2021 (T0), arXiv:2110.08207 §4, §6 · bibliography

The Flan Collection: how to mix the tasks

  • Mixed prompt formats: training on zero-shot, few-shot and chain-of-thought templates together improved every setting; adding 10% few-shot templates raised zero-shot results by 2% or more.
  • Input inversion (asking for the input given the output) did little for held-in tasks but much for held-out: without it, MMLU fell from 50.3 to 41.7 zero-shot.
  • Balance: weighting the mixtures beat an equal mix (MMLU 48.1 against 47.3).
  • Result: Flan-T5 beat earlier open collections by 3–17%; Flan-T5-XL (3B) beat OPT-IML-Max 175B.
  • A better starting point: fine-tuning Flan-T5 on a single task converged faster and peaked higher than fine-tuning T5.
Longpre et al. 2023 (Flan Collection), arXiv:2301.13688 §3–4, Tables 1–2 · bibliography

Training impact and afterlife

  • The recipe stuck: SFT is the same next-token loss, on instructions, with the loss on the answer only (deck 15, architecture deck 11).
  • The data changed: templated academic tasks give short, formulaic answers. Chat models moved to open-ended prompts and long answers written by people or by models (deck 14), with academic sets kept as one ingredient.
  • Lessons that carried over: diversity of tasks and formats matters more than any one dataset; mixing in chain of thought keeps reasoning; a little instruction tuning costs a fraction of a percent of pre-training.
