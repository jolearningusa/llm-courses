Capstone: plan a training run

The capstone puts the course together. You get a compute, data and memory budget and plan the training of a chat and reasoning model from scratch: the token budget and data mixture, the parallelism and precision, the optimizer, the SFT, preference and RL stages, and the evaluations that decide when each stage is done. Every choice is defended with a paper from the course, and the budget is computed with the lab L0 tools.

Step 1: the compute budget

C = 1,024 × 989e12 × 0.40 × 30 days
≈ 1.05 × 10²⁴ FLOPs
  • Chinchilla-optimal at 20 tokens per parameter: about 94B parameters on 1.9T tokens (architecture deck 06).
  • But serving on one GPU argues for a smaller model trained far past that point: an 8B model on 15T tokens takes 6ND ≈ 7.2 × 10²³ FLOPs, about 20.6 days at 40% MFU.
  • That leaves about 9 days for long-context extension, post-training, evaluation and restarts. Decide how many, and show the arithmetic.

python budget.py --params 8e9 --tokens 15e12 --gpus 1024 --peak 989e12 --mfu 0.40

Step 4: optimizer, schedule, context length

  • Optimizer: AdamW or Muon, with the evidence for your choice at your scale (01 Training loop, 04 Muon).
  • Schedule: cosine or warm-up-stable-decay; WSD lets you branch a decay for mid-training and keep going (deck 01).
  • Batch size and learning rate across the run, and whether you tune on a small proxy with μP (deck 02).
  • Stability: QK-norm, z-loss, what you do after a loss spike (03 Training stability).
  • Long context: when and how you extend it, and with how many tokens (11 Long context).

Step 5: post-training

  • SFT: data sources and size, chat template, loss masking and packing (decks 13–15, lab L3).
  • Preferences: where the pairs come from with no preference data in hand (AI feedback, deck 21; on-policy pairs, deck 24), and DPO or PPO (decks 19, 22).
  • RL with verifiable rewards: tasks, verifiers, GRPO settings and length control (decks 25–28).
  • Distillation: would a smaller model distilled from yours serve better (decks 29–30)?
  • Safety: how much safety data, and how you measure over-refusal (deck 31).

What to hand in: a plan plus execution evidence

Mark proposed versus executed work. Keep validation used for selection separate from final testing.

Environment evidence + reporting worksheet · Executed manifest
  • A plan of at most six pages: the six steps above, each choice with the paper (deck and table) that supports it.
  • The budget computed with lab L0: FLOPs, days per stage, memory per GPU, and the margin left for failures.
  • Risks: the three things most likely to go wrong (a loss spike, data running out, reward hacking …), how you would notice each, and what you would do.
  • A presentation in week 15, defending the plan against questions.

A strong plan is consistent: its token budget fits its days, its memory fits its GPUs, and each evaluation gate tests what its stage was meant to change.

Check yourself

  1. Why might a 94B model trained on 1.9T tokens be the wrong answer to this brief, although it is compute-optimal?
  2. Which ZeRO stage is the smallest that fits, and what does a higher stage cost you?
  3. With no preference data, which two sources of pairs would you use first, and which deck supports each?
  4. What evaluation would tell you that RL narrowed the model instead of improving it?
  5. Your SFT model refuses too often. Which knob do you turn, and what do you measure?
