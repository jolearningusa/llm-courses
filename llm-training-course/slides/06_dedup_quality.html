Deduplication and quality filtering

Once the web is text, two questions decide what the model reads: which documents are copies, and which are worth learning from. Lee et al. measure what duplicates do to a model; FineWeb and DataComp-LM find which filters actually train better models.

Problem: copies and junk at web scale

  • One 61-word sentence appears 61,036 times in C4's training set; one near-duplicate cluster holds 250,933 documents.
  • Duplicates waste compute, are memorized, and leak into evaluation sets.
  • "Quality" has no definition: rules catch menus and spam, but which of two well-formed pages teaches more? The only reliable test is to train on each and compare.

Method: exact substrings and MinHash

  • ExactSubstr: a suffix array over the whole corpus finds every repeated span; spans of 50 or more tokens count as duplicates. About 8 bytes per token of memory.
  • NearDup (MinHash): represent a document by its set of 5-grams; k hash functions each keep their minimum; two documents agree on a minimum with probability equal to their Jaccard similarity. Lee et al.: 9,000 hashes, 20 bands of 450, then confirm Jaccard and edit similarity above 0.8.
  • Bands (LSH): documents that match on every row of any one band become candidates, so only candidates are compared.
Lee et al. 2021, arXiv:2107.06499 §4, App. A–B · bibliography

How bands set the threshold

With b bands of r rows, P(candidate) = 1 − (1 − sr)b. More rows push the curve right (stricter), more bands push it left. FineWeb's 14 × 8 targets documents about 75% similar or more. Lab L2 checks the formula and the curve.

FineWeb: deduplicate per snapshot, then filter

  • 15 trillion tokens from 96 Common Crawl snapshots, every choice tested by training 1.71B models on 28B tokens (filters) or 350B (dedup).
  • A surprise in dedup: MinHash across all snapshots kept about 4T tokens and trained worse models than RefinedWeb; the data that survived global dedup had more ads and keyword lists than what it removed. Deduplicating each snapshot on its own kept about 20T tokens and matched RefinedWeb.
  • Three new line rules, about 22% of tokens removed, about +1% on the aggregate: lines ending in punctuation ≤ 0.12; characters in duplicated lines ≥ 0.1; lines under 30 characters ≥ 0.67. Lab L2 implements all three.
Penedo et al. 2024 (FineWeb), arXiv:2406.17557 §3.1, §3.4, §3.6, Figs. 3–5 · bibliography

FineWeb-Edu: a classifier trained on LLM labels

  • Llama-3-70B-Instruct scored 460,000 pages for educational value from 0 to 5.
  • A linear regressor on text embeddings learned those scores; pages scoring 3 or more were kept: 1.3T tokens. Scoring all of FineWeb took 6,000 H100 hours.
  • MMLU 33% → 37%, ARC 46% → 57% against FineWeb at the same budget.
  • FineWeb-Edu reached 33.6% MMLU at 38B tokens; another open corpus (Matrix) needed about 300B tokens for the same score.
Penedo et al. 2024 (FineWeb), arXiv:2406.17557 §4, Figs. 11, 17 · bibliography

DataComp-LM: hold the model fixed, vary the data

  • A 240T-token pool from Common Crawl, a fixed training recipe and five compute scales from 412M to 6.9B parameters. Rankings at 1B correlate with 7B rankings at r = 0.956.
  • Model-based filtering wins: a fastText bigram classifier beat perplexity filtering and the RefinedWeb rules (30.2 against 29.0 and 27.5 on the core score at 1B).
  • The best classifier's positives were instruction data (OpenHermes 2.5) and r/ExplainLikeImFive posts; negatives were ordinary filtered web pages. Keeping the top 10% beat 15% and 20%.
  • MinHash and a Bloom-filter dedup were within 0.2 points; the Bloom filter was kept because it scales past 10 TB.
