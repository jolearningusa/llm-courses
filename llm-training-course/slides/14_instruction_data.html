Instruction data: Self-Instruct, Alpaca and LIMA

Templated NLP tasks (deck 13) do not look like what people ask a chat model. Self-Instruct let a model write its own instructions; Alpaca used that recipe to make a 7B chat model for under $600; LIMA showed that 1,000 carefully chosen examples can be enough.

Problem: open-ended demonstrations are expensive

  • Writing thousands of varied requests with good answers by hand is slow and costly; InstructGPT's demonstrations came from hired labelers.
  • Academic datasets cover classification and short answers, not "write a cover letter" or "explain this error".
  • Two open questions: can a model generate the data, and how much data does SFT actually need?

Self-Instruct: bootstrapping from 175 seed tasks

GPT-3 (davinci) writes new instructions from examples in the pool, then inputs and outputs for each. For classification tasks it writes the label first so the classes stay balanced. A new instruction is kept only if its ROUGE-L overlap with every existing one is below 0.7. Result: 52,445 instructions and 82,439 instances.

Alpaca: Self-Instruct with a stronger teacher

  • LLaMA 7B fine-tuned on 52K demonstrations generated by text-davinci-003, starting from the same 175 seed tasks.
  • Cost: under $500 for the data through the API, and 3 hours on 8 A100s (under $100) for the fine-tune.
  • Evaluation: in a blind pairwise comparison by five of the authors, Alpaca won 90 comparisons and text-davinci-003 89.
  • Limits stated: hallucination, toxicity and stereotypes; research use only, under LLaMA's license and the API's terms.

Evidence: LIMA

  • Human raters judged LIMA equivalent or better than GPT-4 in 43% of cases, Bard in 58% and DaVinci003 in 65%.
  • Quality: filtered Stack Exchange beat unfiltered by about 0.5 points on the helpfulness scale.
  • Diversity: varied Stack Exchange prompts beat homogeneous wikiHow "how to" prompts at the same size.
  • Quantity: growing Stack Exchange data 16-fold, from 2K to 32K examples, did not improve answers.
  • Dialogue: adding 30 multi-turn chains raised excellent responses in dialogue from 45.2% to 76.1%.

Training impact and afterlife

  • Synthetic SFT data won. Generating prompts and answers with a strong model became the default way to build instruction sets; Tülu 3's mix is about 30% persona-driven synthetic prompts (deck 15).
  • Quality and diversity over volume, with a caveat: LIMA measured style and helpfulness. Skills such as math and code still grow with targeted data (deck 15's ablations).
  • Imitation has a ceiling. A student copies its teacher's style faster than its knowledge; preference tuning and RL (Parts VII to IX) push past it.
  • Licenses and terms travel with generated data, as Alpaca's research-only release shows.
