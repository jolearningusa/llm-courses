RL with verifiable rewards: DeepSeek-R1, Kimi k1.5 and Tülu 3

Tülu 3 (2024) named RL with verifiable rewards (RLVR): reward a model only when a program confirms its answer. DeepSeek-R1-Zero (2025) applied it with GRPO directly to a base model, and long chains of thought, with checking and backtracking, emerged without being taught. DeepSeek-R1 and Kimi k1.5 turned that into full training pipelines.

Problem: a reward model can be gamed, a checker much less

  • Reward models are proxies, and policies exploit them (deck 20). For long training runs that is the limit.
  • Math answers, unit tests and format rules can be checked by a program. A checked reward is exact on the task, so RL can run much longer.
  • Questions: does RL on a checker teach reasoning, or only sharpen what the base model knows (deck 26's Maj@K finding)? And what else does a full model need?

DeepSeek-R1-Zero: RL straight from the base model

  • GRPO on DeepSeek-V3-Base with no SFT. Rewards: accuracy (a rule checks the answer or runs tests) and format (reasoning inside <think> tags). No neural reward model, because of reward hacking.
  • A plain template asks for reasoning then an answer, and says nothing about how to reason.
  • AIME 2024 pass@1 rose from 15.6% to 77.9% during training, 86.7% with majority voting. Answers grew steadily longer: the model learned to spend more tokens on hard problems.
  • An "aha moment": mid-solution, the model wrote that it should re-evaluate, and backtracked. Reflection and verification were not in any training data.
  • Drawbacks: poor readability and mixed languages.

DeepSeek-R1: four stages

  • A small cold start of readable long chains of thought fixes R1-Zero's format. A language-consistency reward during reasoning RL stops language mixing, at a small cost in accuracy.
  • Rejection sampling from the RL checkpoint, plus non-reasoning data (writing, QA), gives about 800K SFT examples; a second RL stage adds reward models for helpfulness and harmlessness.
  • Results: AIME 2024 79.8%, MATH-500 97.3%, Codeforces rating 2,029. Model architecture: architecture deck 31.

Kimi k1.5: long context instead of extra machinery

  • RL with a 128K-token context. Partial rollouts cap each generation at a budget and resume unfinished ones in the next iteration.
  • Policy optimization is a variant of online mirror descent with a relative-entropy term; the baseline is the mean reward of k samples. No value network, no Monte Carlo tree search, no process reward model.
  • A length reward, normalized among each problem's samples, favours short correct answers; curriculum and prioritized sampling (by 1 − success rate) pick problems.
  • Results: AIME 2024 77.5, MATH-500 96.2, Codeforces 94th percentile. Long2short (merging, shortest rejection sampling, DPO, length-penalized RL) gave a short-answer model with AIME 60.8 at about 3,300 tokens.

Training impact and afterlife

  • Reasoning models became a category: thinking tokens before the answer, trained with RL on checkable tasks, with test-time compute as a dial (deck 28).
  • Cheap replications followed on smaller bases, which raised the question of how much the base model already contained (Dr. GRPO, deck 28).
  • Distillation carries it down: R1's samples fine-tuned small Qwen and Llama models into reasoning models (deck 30).
  • The limit: tasks without a reliable checker, such as writing, still need reward models, as R1's own limitations note.
