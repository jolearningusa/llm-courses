AlexNet and VGG

Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton, 2012, and Simonyan and Zisserman, 2014. A much larger LeNet, trained on 1.2 million labelled images with two GPUs, ReLUs and dropout, cut the ImageNet error rate by about 40% and started the deep learning era. Two years later VGG showed that simply going deeper with small filters went further.

Problem: natural images at scale

  • ImageNet's ILSVRC classification task: 1000 classes, about 1.2 million training images, scored by top-5 error (correct if the true label is among five guesses).
  • Before 2012 the best systems used hand-designed features (SIFT, Fisher vectors) and linear classifiers.
  • CNNs were known to work on digits (deck 12), but training one large enough for natural images needed more data, more compute and ways to stop it overfitting.
Russakovsky et al. 2014, arXiv:1409.0575, §1 and §3 · Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton 2012, NeurIPS, §1 · bibliography

AlexNet: the network

  • Five convolutional layers then three fully connected layers and a 1000-way softmax: 60 million parameters and 650,000 neurons. First layer: 96 filters of 11 × 11 at stride 4 on 224 × 224 inputs.
  • ReLU instead of tanh: a four-layer CNN reached 25% training error on CIFAR-10 six times faster (deck 05).
  • Two GPUs (3 GB each): the network was split across them, with connections between the two halves only in some layers. Training took five to six days.
  • Overlapping max pooling and local response normalization each improved top-1 error by 0.3 to 1.4 points; later networks dropped the latter.
Krizhevsky, Sutskever and Hinton 2012, NeurIPS, §3, Figures 1 and 2 · bibliography

AlexNet: keeping 60 million parameters from overfitting

  • Data augmentation: random 224 × 224 crops of 256 × 256 images and their mirror images (a factor of 2048 more training patterns), and a color perturbation along the principal components of RGB values.
  • Dropout with probability 0.5 in the first two fully connected layers. Without it the network overfit substantially; with it, it took about twice as many iterations to converge.
  • Training: SGD with batch 128, momentum 0.9, weight decay 0.0005, learning rate 0.01 divided by 10 when validation error stopped improving, three times in total.
  • Every item on this slide is from decks 08 to 10; this paper is why they became standard.

VGG: depth with 3 × 3 filters

VGG uses only 3 × 3 convolutions (stride 1, padding 1) and 2 × 2 max pooling, doubling channels after each pool from 64 to 512. Stacks of small filters cover the same receptive field as one large filter with fewer weights and more nonlinearities.

Simonyan and Zisserman 2014, arXiv:1409.1556, §2.1 and §2.3 · bibliography

VGG: what depth bought

  • Configurations from 11 to 19 weight layers. On ILSVRC validation, error fell steadily with depth up to 16 and 19 layers, then saturated.
  • VGG-16 has about 138 million parameters, most of them in the first fully connected layer (7 × 7 × 512 → 4096).
  • Deeper configurations were trained by initializing them from shallower ones; the authors note afterwards that Glorot initialization (deck 07) made that unnecessary.
  • ILSVRC-2014: second in classification (7.3% top-5 test error at submission, 6.8% with two models afterwards) and first in localization.
  • The uniform design made VGG the standard feature extractor for years, for transfer learning, detection and style transfer.
Simonyan and Zisserman 2014, arXiv:1409.1556, §3, §4 and Tables 2–7 · bibliography

Then depth stopped helping

  • VGG's error saturated at 19 layers. He et al. then showed that adding more plain layers made networks worse, and not from overfitting: the training error went up too.
  • Batch normalization (deck 11) and good initialization (deck 07) fixed the vanishing signal at the start of training, yet the problem remained.
  • Lab L4 reproduces it at small scale: a 64-layer plain MLP on the spirals stays at the chance-level loss log 3 = 1.10, while an 8-layer one reaches 0.025.
  • Residual connections (deck 14) removed this limit.

Where this shows up in LLMs

  • The AlexNet recipe, a big model, a big dataset, GPUs, and regularization for the remaining gap, is the same bet that LLM scaling made a decade later.
  • Splitting a model across devices because it does not fit on one, as AlexNet did on two GPUs, is now model parallelism (tensor and pipeline parallelism) in every large training run.
  • VGG's lesson that a uniform stack of one simple block, made deeper, beats elaborate designs is the Transformer's design too: N identical blocks.
  • Pre-trained feature extractors fine-tuned for new tasks are the ancestors of pre-trained language models.
