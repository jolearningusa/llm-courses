Attention in sequence to sequence

Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio, 2014, "Neural Machine Translation by Jointly Learning to Align and Translate", and Luong, Pham and Manning, 2015. Instead of squeezing the source into one vector, keep all the encoder states and let the decoder, at each step, compute a weighted average of them with weights it chooses. Translation quality stopped falling with sentence length, and the weights turned out to be word alignments.

Problem: one vector for the whole sentence

  • Deck 17: the encoder–decoder passes the source through a fixed-size vector, and quality falls as sentences get longer. In lab L6 whole-string accuracy falls from 100% at length 4 to 1% at length 16.
  • Bahdanau et al. called this the bottleneck and proposed to free the network from it: the encoder keeps one vector per source word, and the decoder looks up the ones it needs for each target word.
  • The lookup must be differentiable, so it can be trained with the rest of the network by backpropagation: a soft weighting, not a hard choice.
Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio 2014, arXiv:1409.0473, §1 and §3 · bibliography

The mechanism

eij = score(si−1, hj) = vaT tanh(Wasi−1 + Uahj)
αij = softmaxj(eij) , ci = Σj αij hj

  • hj: the encoder's annotation of source word j. si−1: the decoder's state before writing target word i.
  • The scores are turned into a distribution over source positions (deck 02's softmax); the context ci is their weighted average and is fed to the decoder with the previous word.
  • The score is a small MLP, "additive" attention. Its cost is one score per pair (i, j): n·m in all.
Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio 2014, arXiv:1409.0473, §3.1, equations 4–6 · bibliography

Bahdanau et al.: the model

  • A bidirectional encoder: one GRU reads the sentence forwards, one backwards, and hj joins their states, so each annotation describes the words on both sides of j.
  • 1,000 hidden units in each direction and in the decoder; 30,000-word vocabularies; WMT'14 English to French, 348 million words after data selection.
  • Two versions of each model: trained on sentences of up to 30 words ("-30") and up to 50 words ("-50"). Minibatches of 80 sentences, Adadelta, about five days per model; beam search at test time.
Bahdanau, Cho and Bengio 2014, arXiv:1409.0473, §3.2, §4 and Appendix A · bibliography

Luong et al.: simpler and stronger

  • Score with the current decoder state ht: dot htTh̄s, general htTWah̄s, or concat (Bahdanau's MLP). Then combine: h̃t = tanh(Wc[ct; ht]) feeds the softmax.
  • Global attention looks at every source word; local attention predicts a position and looks at a window of ±10 words. Input feeding passes h̃t into the next step, so the model knows what it attended to before.
  • English to German, WMT'14: a 4-layer LSTM went from 14.0 BLEU without attention to 16.8 with global (location-based) attention and 18.1 with input feeding. An ensemble set a new state of the art on WMT'15, 25.9 BLEU. Dot scores worked best for global attention.
Luong, Pham and Manning 2015, arXiv:1508.04025, §3, Tables 1 and 2 · bibliography

What the attention learned

Over 500 strings of length 12, 81% of the weight is on the digit just written and 8% on the one being written. With a forward-only encoder, the state at T − t has just read digit T − 1 − t, so the decoder finds its place by matching what it wrote and reads the neighbour. The alignment is learned, and it need not be the one a person would draw.

Where this shows up in LLMs

  • The Transformer keeps exactly this computation and drops the recurrence: every position scores every other with scaled dot products, takes a softmax and averages. Queries, keys and values name the three roles that s, h for scoring and h for averaging play here.
  • Luong's dot score, divided by √d, is the Transformer's; Bahdanau's alignment heatmaps became the attention maps people inspect in LLMs.
  • "Find the earlier position whose token matches, then copy what came after it" is what induction heads do, the circuit Olsson et al. link to in-context learning.
  • Google's 2016 production translation system was an 8-layer LSTM encoder–decoder with attention, cutting errors by about 60% against its phrase-based system. The Transformer replaced it a year later.
Vaswani et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.03762 · Olsson et al. 2022, arXiv:2209.11895 · Wu et al. 2016, arXiv:1609.08144 · bibliography
