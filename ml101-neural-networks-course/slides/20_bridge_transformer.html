What still held RNNs back

Per layer, sequence length n, width dOperationsSequential stepsLongest path between two positions
RecurrentO(n·d²)O(n)O(n)

Self-attentionO(n²·d)O(1)O(1)
  • Attention had fixed the bottleneck (deck 18), but the encoder and decoder were still RNNs: step t waits for step t − 1, so a sentence cannot be processed in parallel on a GPU, and information from far back still passes through many steps.
  • Self-attention connects any two positions in one step and computes all positions at once. Its cost grows with n², which is cheaper than an RNN while n < d.

Self-attention is deck 18 without the RNN

Attention(Q, K, V) = softmax(Q KT / √dk) V

  • Each position projects its vector three ways: a query (what it looks for), a key (what it offers to be found by) and a value (what it passes on). In deck 18 the decoder state was the query and the encoder states were both keys and values.
  • The score is Luong's dot product, divided by √dk so its variance does not grow with the width (the same argument as deck 07's initialization).
  • Multi-head: several attentions with smaller projections in parallel, so a position can look for different things at once.
  • In a decoder, a mask stops position t from attending to later positions, so training with teacher forcing (deck 17) stays honest and runs over all positions at once.

What is new: order and scale

  • Order. An RNN knows the order of its inputs because it reads them in order. Attention does not: shuffle the inputs and the outputs shuffle with them. The Transformer adds a positional encoding to each token vector; later models rotate queries and keys instead (RoPE, Transformer course deck 08).
  • Tokens. Inputs are sub-word tokens, a vocabulary of tens of thousands of pieces between characters and words (Transformer course deck 07).
  • Scale. Parallel training made models with billions of parameters, trained on trillions of tokens, practical. How loss falls with model size, data and compute became a science of its own (scaling laws, Transformer course decks 05 and 06).

Recurrence comes back

  • At generation time a Transformer stores every past key and value (the KV cache), which grows with the context. An RNN keeps a fixed-size state.
  • Katharopoulos et al. showed that attention with the softmax replaced by a kernel feature map can be computed as an RNN whose state is a d × d matrix: "Transformers are RNNs".
  • Modern recurrent layers (Mamba, Gated DeltaNet) add input-dependent gates to that state, the LSTM idea of deck 16, and keep training parallel with a scan over the sequence. Hybrid models mix them with a few attention layers (Transformer course decks 23 to 25).
  • The question this course followed, how to carry information across a long sequence without losing the gradient, is still open.
