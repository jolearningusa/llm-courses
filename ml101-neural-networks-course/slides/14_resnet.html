ResNet and residual connections

He, Zhang, Ren and Sun, 2015, "Deep Residual Learning for Image Recognition". Let each block learn a correction to its input instead of a whole new representation: y = x + F(x). With that one change, networks went from about 20 layers to 152 on ImageNet and over 1000 on CIFAR-10, and the residual connection became part of every deep architecture since, the Transformer included.

Problem: deeper plain networks train worse

  • On CIFAR-10, a 56-layer plain network had higher training error than a 20-layer one. Not overfitting, and not vanishing gradients at the start either: the networks used batch normalization and good initialization.
  • A deeper network could, in principle, copy the shallower one and set its extra layers to the identity. The optimizer just does not find that solution: this is the degradation problem.
  • On ImageNet the same held: plain-34 had higher validation error than plain-18 (28.54% against 27.94% top-1).

Change: learn the residual

If the best mapping is close to the identity, F only has to be close to zero, which is easy: small weights do it. The shortcut is free (no parameters, no extra computation beyond an addition). When the shape changes, a 1 × 1 convolution with stride 2 projects x.

Why it helps the gradient

xL = xl + Σi=l..L−1 F(xi) ⇒ ∂ℓ/∂xl = ∂ℓ/∂xL · (1 + ∂/∂xl Σ F)

  • With identity shortcuts, any deep layer's output is a shallow layer's output plus a sum of residuals, and the gradient reaching the shallow layer contains the top gradient unchanged (the "1"). It cannot vanish through a product of many small factors, as in deck 07.
  • He et al.'s follow-up showed this works best when the shortcut path is kept clean: move batch norm and ReLU inside the residual branch ("pre-activation"), so nothing but additions lies on the path from input to output. A 1001-layer network trained on CIFAR-10 to 4.62% error.
He et al. 2016, arXiv:1603.05027, §2, §3 and §4 · bibliography

The architecture

  • ResNet-34: a 7 × 7 convolution, then stacks of the two-layer 3 × 3 block, halving resolution and doubling channels (64 to 512) four times, then global average pooling and one fully connected layer. Batch norm after every convolution.
  • Deeper versions (50, 101, 152 layers) use a bottleneck block: 1 × 1 to reduce channels, 3 × 3, 1 × 1 to expand again, so each block costs about the same as a two-layer block.
  • ResNet-152 has fewer FLOPs (11.3 billion) than VGG-16/19 (15.3/19.6 billion).
  • Training: SGD with momentum 0.9, batch 256, weight decay 0.0001, learning rate 0.1 divided by 10 when the error plateaus, no dropout.
He et al. 2015, arXiv:1512.03385, §3.3, §3.4 and §4.1, Table 1 · bibliography

  • Residual networks get better with depth where plain ones get worse. ResNet-152 reached 4.49% single-model top-5 validation error, and an ensemble won ILSVRC 2015 with 3.57% top-5 test error.
  • On CIFAR-10 a 110-layer ResNet reached 6.43% error. A 1202-layer one still trained (training error below 0.1%) but tested worse, 7.93%: the authors attribute it to overfitting a small dataset.
  • The same ResNet features also won the 2015 detection and localization competitions.

Before and after

  • Highway networks (Srivastava et al., 2015) had shortcut connections controlled by learned gates, y = T(x)·H(x) + (1 − T(x))·x, and trained networks with 100 layers. ResNet's shortcut is the special case with no gate: always open, no parameters.
  • The LSTM's cell state (deck 16) is the same idea over time: an additive path the gradient can follow across many steps.
  • After ResNet, almost every deep architecture uses residual connections: DenseNet, U-Net variants, and above all the Transformer.
Srivastava, Greff and Schmidhuber 2015, arXiv:1505.00387 · He et al. 2015, arXiv:1512.03385, §2 · bibliography

Where this shows up in LLMs

  • Every Transformer sub-layer is wrapped in a residual connection: x + Attention(x), then x + FFN(x). The 2017 paper cites ResNet for it.
  • With Pre-LN (Transformer course deck 17), the stream of additions from the embedding to the output is never normalized: it is the clean shortcut path of the pre-activation ResNet. People call it the residual stream, and each block reads from it and writes a correction into it.
  • That is why 100-block LLMs train at all, and why initializations scale down the output of each residual branch with depth.
Vaswani et al. 2017, arXiv:1706.03762, §3.1 · Transformer course, deck 17 · bibliography
