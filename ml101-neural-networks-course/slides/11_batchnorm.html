Batch normalization

Ioffe and Szegedy, 2015, "Batch Normalization: Accelerating Deep Network Training by Reducing Internal Covariate Shift". Standardize each layer's inputs using the statistics of the current mini-batch, then let the network rescale them. Deep networks became much less sensitive to initialization and learning rate, and trained in far fewer steps.

Problem: every layer chases a moving target

  • Deck 07 kept activations at a good scale at initialization. During training the weights change, and so does the distribution each layer receives from the layers below.
  • The authors called this internal covariate shift and argued it forces small learning rates and careful initialization, and makes saturating activations hard to train.
  • Inputs to the whole network were already standardized as standard practice. The idea: do the same for the input of every layer, inside the network, as part of the model.
Ioffe and Szegedy 2015, arXiv:1502.03167, §1 and §2 · bibliography

The transform

x̂ = (x − μB) / √(σ²B + ε) , y = γ x̂ + β

  • For each feature, μB and σ²B are the mean and variance over the mini-batch. For convolutional layers, the statistics are shared over the batch and all spatial positions of a channel.
  • γ and β are learned per feature. They let the layer undo the normalization if that is what training wants (γ = σ, β = μ gives back the input), so it does not limit what the network can represent.
  • The bias of the layer before is redundant: subtracting the mean removes it (in lab L3 its gradient is exactly zero).
  • Placed between the linear map and the nonlinearity in the original paper.
Ioffe and Szegedy 2015, arXiv:1502.03167, §3, Algorithm 1 and §3.2 · bibliography
3 / 10

Training and inference behave differently

  • During training, every example's output depends on the other examples in its batch, and the gradient flows through μB and σ²B too (the extra terms in lab L3's BatchNorm.backward).
  • At inference the output must depend on the example alone. Keep running averages of μ and σ² during training and use them instead.
  • Forgetting to switch modes (model.eval() in PyTorch) is a classic bug: predictions change with the batch they are in. A lab L3 check verifies that in evaluation mode an example's output is the same alone or in a batch.
  • Very small batches give noisy statistics and hurt; so do batches that are not representative (sorted data, one class per batch).

What the paper showed

  • On ImageNet, an Inception network with batch norm reached the original model's accuracy in 14 times fewer training steps.
  • It allowed much higher learning rates (up to 30 times in their variants), made dropout less necessary, and made training with sigmoid nonlinearities possible again.
  • An ensemble of batch-normalized networks reached 4.9% top-5 validation error on ImageNet, better than the best published result at the time.
  • Batch norm became standard in convolutional networks; ResNet (deck 14) puts it after every convolution.
Ioffe and Szegedy 2015, arXiv:1502.03167, §4.2 · bibliography

Why does it work? Not quite the original story

  • Santurkar et al. added noise after batch norm that deliberately shifted each layer's input distribution at every step. The networks trained about as well as with plain batch norm, so reducing internal covariate shift cannot be the main reason.
  • They found instead that batch norm makes the loss landscape smoother: the loss and its gradients change more slowly as the weights move (better Lipschitz constants), so larger steps are safe and the gradient is more predictive.
  • A side effect the original authors noted: each example is normalized with statistics of a random batch, which adds noise and acts as a mild regularizer.
Santurkar et al. 2018, arXiv:1805.11604, §2 and §3 · Ioffe and Szegedy 2015, arXiv:1502.03167, §3.4 · bibliography

Layer normalization and the batch problem

Batch statistics are awkward for recurrent networks (a different batch at each time step), tiny batches and per-token generation. Layer norm computes the mean and variance over the features of one example instead: no batch dependence, the same computation in training and inference.

Ba, Kiros and Hinton 2016, arXiv:1607.06450, §3 · bibliography
8 / 10

Where this shows up in LLMs

  • Transformers use layer norm, not batch norm: sequences have different lengths, generation processes one token at a time, and the statistics must not leak between examples.
  • The 2017 Transformer normalized after each residual addition (Post-LN); GPT-2 and most later models normalize the input of each sub-layer (Pre-LN), and many use RMSNorm, which drops the mean subtraction and β (Transformer course deck 17).
  • QK-norm normalizes queries and keys inside attention for the same purpose: keep scales bounded so large learning rates stay stable.
  • Batch norm's legacy is the idea: put normalization inside the network and give it learned scale and shift parameters.
Transformer course, deck 17 · Ba, Kiros and Hinton 2016, arXiv:1607.06450 · bibliography
