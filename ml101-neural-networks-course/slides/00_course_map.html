ML 101: neural network foundations

The machine learning that comes before the Transformer: linear and logistic models, gradient descent and backpropagation, multilayer networks, the optimizers and regularizers that make them train, convolutional and recurrent networks, and the attention mechanism that led to the Transformer.

Why this course

  • Modern LLMs reuse almost every idea in this course: a softmax output and a cross-entropy loss, backpropagation, Adam with warm-up and decay, weight decay and dropout, residual connections and normalization, and attention.
  • Reading a Transformer paper is far easier once each of those is familiar on a small model where you can see every number.
  • The course builds each idea from first principles, implements it in a lab small enough to run on a laptop CPU, and ends each deck with where the idea shows up in LLMs.
  • It is the prerequisite for the LLM Transformer Architecture course, which starts with the 2017 Transformer.

One loop, six parts

Part I fits the simplest models with this loop. Part II makes the model deep. Part III makes the update and the loss behave. Parts IV and V specialize the model to images and sequences. Part VI is how to run the loop in practice, and where it leads.

How each deck is built

  1. The idea: the problem it solves, in a sentence or two.
  2. The mechanics: the equations, derived where they are short.
  3. A worked example: numbers measured in the course's own labs, not quoted from elsewhere.
  4. The traps: what goes wrong in practice and how to see it.
  5. Where it shows up in LLMs: the same idea in a modern language model.
  6. Check yourself and lab: four questions and the lab that goes with the deck.

Decks that follow one paper (backpropagation, initialization, Adam, batch normalization, ResNet, LSTM, attention) also say what the paper showed and cite its sections.

Prerequisites and scope

  • You need: Python and NumPy; linear algebra (matrix products, transposes, eigenvalues); calculus to the chain rule and partial derivatives; basic probability (mean, variance, likelihood).
  • In scope: supervised learning with linear models, MLPs, CNNs and RNNs; how to optimize, regularize and debug them.
  • Out of scope here: the Transformer and its variants (the Transformer Architecture course), LLM pre-training at scale and post-training such as instruction tuning and RLHF (LLM Training and Post-Training), and classical methods beyond what neural networks need (decision trees, SVMs, kernels, clustering).

How to study it

  • One part per week or two: read the decks, do the lab, answer the check-yourself questions without looking.
  • In every lab, change one thing at a time and predict the result before you run it.
  • Keep a notebook of the numbers you measure: initial loss, the learning rate that diverges, the gap between training and validation. They are the intuition the course is trying to build.
  • The final deck maps each part onto a Transformer block. Read it before starting the Transformer course.
